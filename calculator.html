How the reconstitution math works

Concentration is how much compound ends up in each millilitre of solution: the amount in the vial divided by the BAC (bacteriostatic) water you add. More water means a weaker mix and a bigger, easier-to-read draw; less water means a stronger mix and a smaller draw.

Units to draw is the number that matters at the needle. Insulin syringes are U-100, meaning 100 units per millilitre — so once you know the volume for your dose, the unit mark is just that volume × 100. A 1cc, 0.5cc, and 0.3cc syringe all use the same scale; they only differ in how much they hold.

Doses per vial tells you how long a vial lasts at your dose, so you can reorder before you run dry. For HGH the calculator uses the standard 1 mg = 3 IU conversion; for HCG and peptides it works directly from the IU or mg you enter.
