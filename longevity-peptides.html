Longevity Peptides

Compounds studied for the biology of aging — mitochondria, cellular senescence, telomeres, and cellular repair. This guide sorts the real categories from the hype, is honest about how much is still preclinical, and explains what each one is actually being studied for.

What are longevity peptides?

"Longevity peptides" is a loose umbrella for compounds studied for their effect on the machinery of aging rather than any single disease. The appeal is targeting an upstream process — energy production, cellular cleanup, DNA protection — in the hope of broad downstream benefits. The catch is that aging is extraordinarily complex, and most of what's known about these compounds comes from cells and animals, not from long-term human studies.

The category is not one chemical family. It's a grouping by goal, and it spans several mechanisms:

  • Mitochondrial-derived peptides — short peptides encoded within mitochondrial DNA, like MOTS-c and humanin, studied for metabolism and cellular stress resistance.
  • Mitochondria-targeting peptides — compounds like SS-31 (elamipretide) and SS-20 that concentrate in mitochondria to support energy production.
  • Senolytics — compounds like FOXO4-DRI studied for clearing "senescent" cells that accumulate with age.
  • Telomere & pineal peptides — epitalon and pinealon, short peptides studied for telomerase activity, circadian rhythm, and cognition.
  • Longevity-protein-based compounds — such as the Klotho peptide and GDF11, derived from proteins tied to aging.
  • Supportive non-peptides & blends — NAD+ (a coenzyme, not a peptide) and combination blends like GLOW and KLOW round out most real-world protocols.

One honest caveat up front: nearly everything here is research-only, much of the evidence is preclinical, and at least one entry (GDF11's role in aging) is genuinely contested among scientists. Treat this as a map of what's being studied — not a list of proven therapies.

How they're commonly used & stacked

Most of these are lyophilized peptides, reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and injected subcutaneously; a few (NAD+, blends) also appear as oral, nasal, or topical forms. They're typically referenced in cycles rather than taken indefinitely — for example a defined run of epitalon once or twice a year, or a loading phase of NAD+. StackMindr's free reconstitution calculator handles the draw-volume math for the injectables.

Common pairings you'll see discussed are epitalon with NAD+ as a general "longevity base," a mitochondrial peptide (MOTS-c or SS-31) added for energy and exercise capacity, and the GLOW or KLOW blends layered in for skin and recovery. These combinations are popular in the community but have not been validated in controlled human trials, and none of this is a dosing recommendation — any protocol should be set with a knowledgeable provider.

Longevity Peptides FAQ

What are longevity peptides?

Compounds studied for their effects on the biology of aging — mitochondrial function, cellular senescence, telomeres, and cellular repair. The group is a mix: short peptides like epitalon and pinealon, mitochondrial-derived peptides like MOTS-c and humanin, mitochondria-targeting peptides like SS-31, senolytics like FOXO4-DRI, and a few non-peptides (such as NAD+) that are central to longevity protocols. The common thread is a target in the aging process, not a shared chemical class.

Do longevity peptides actually slow aging?

There's no proof of that in humans. Most of the evidence is preclinical — cells, worms, flies, and mice — and the human data is limited. Some targets are genuinely promising and some, like GDF11's role in aging, are actively contested in the scientific literature. These are research compounds, not proven anti-aging treatments, and almost none are approved medicines.

Which longevity peptide is the most studied?

Epitalon is the flagship — a pineal tetrapeptide studied for telomerase activity and circadian rhythm, with the longest history in the longevity community. The mitochondrial-derived peptides MOTS-c and humanin are the most active areas of mainstream academic research, and SS-31 (elamipretide) has been through human clinical trials for mitochondrial disease.

Are longevity peptides FDA approved?

Almost none are approved as anti-aging treatments. Most are research compounds. NAD+ precursors are sold as supplements rather than approved drugs, and SS-31 (elamipretide) has been investigated in trials but is not approved for general use. Nothing in this guide is a substitute for medical care.
