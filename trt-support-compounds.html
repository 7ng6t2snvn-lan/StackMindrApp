TRT Support Compounds

Testosterone replacement is the core, but it rarely travels alone. This guide covers the testosterone esters themselves plus the compounds discussed alongside them — the ones that protect fertility, the upstream signals, and the research-only muscle and growth-factor compounds — with an honest line drawn between what's approved and what isn't.

What are TRT support compounds?

"TRT support compounds" is a loose umbrella for the medicines and research compounds that come up in conversations about testosterone replacement therapy. At the center are the testosterone esters — cypionate, enanthate, and propionate — which are genuine, FDA-approved treatments for diagnosed low testosterone. Everything else in this guide orbits them.

It's worth separating the group by what it actually is, because the regulatory status is not uniform:

  • Testosterone esters — cypionate, enanthate, propionate. Approved medicines; the difference between them is mostly how long each dose lasts.
  • Axis support — HCG and gonadorelin, used to keep the body's own testicular signal alive during TRT, with legitimate roles in fertility.
  • Upstream signals — kisspeptin-10 and kisspeptin-54, which sit a step further up the chain that drives GnRH release.
  • Research-only muscle & growth factors — follistatin-344, ACE-031, PEG-MGF, IGF-1 LR3, IGF-1 DES, and HGH, often mentioned in the same breath but not approved for this use.

The honest framing matters here: a man on properly prescribed TRT with HCG is in very different territory from someone adding research-only myostatin inhibitors or IGF-1 variants. This guide is educational, not a protocol.

How they're commonly used & stacked

Testosterone esters are oil-based injectables dosed on a schedule tied to their half-life — longer esters like cypionate and enanthate are commonly referenced once or twice weekly, while short-acting propionate is injected more often. HCG and gonadorelin are water-based and run alongside on their own cadence. Keeping all of that straight, and getting draw volumes right from a given concentration, is exactly what StackMindr's free calculator and scheduling are for.

The one choice almost everyone starting TRT runs into is cypionate versus enanthate — functionally close, with only a slight difference in half-life; the testosterone cypionate vs enanthate comparison walks through it. From there, HCG or gonadorelin is commonly added to preserve testicular function. The research-only muscle and growth-factor compounds are a separate world entirely and are not approved for this use. None of this is a dosing recommendation — TRT and anything around it should be managed by a qualified provider with bloodwork.

TRT Support Compounds FAQ

What are TRT support compounds?

The medicines and research compounds discussed alongside testosterone replacement therapy. At the center are the testosterone esters — cypionate, enanthate, and propionate — which are FDA-approved treatments for diagnosed low testosterone. Around them sit compounds used to support the body's own hormonal axis, such as HCG and gonadorelin, which help preserve testicular function and fertility, plus upstream signals like kisspeptin. A separate group of research-only muscle and growth-factor compounds is often mentioned in the same conversations but is not approved.

What is the difference between testosterone cypionate and enanthate?

Both are long-acting injectable testosterone esters used for testosterone replacement. The practical difference is small: cypionate has a slightly longer half-life (around 8 days) and enanthate is slightly shorter (around 7 days). Cypionate is the US standard, while enanthate is more common elsewhere. In practice they're often considered interchangeable, and the choice usually comes down to availability and a provider's preference.

Why are HCG and gonadorelin used with TRT?

When testosterone is replaced from the outside, the body's own signal to the testes drops, which can shrink them and reduce fertility. HCG mimics LH and keeps the testes active, helping preserve size and fertility. Gonadorelin is a GnRH that prompts the pituitary to release LH and FSH, and is used as a shorter-acting alternative. Both have legitimate roles in fertility and TRT support and should be managed by a provider.

Are TRT support compounds FDA approved?

It's a split. Testosterone cypionate, enanthate, and propionate are FDA-approved medicines for diagnosed low testosterone, and HCG and gonadorelin have approved and legitimate medical uses. The myostatin, activin, and IGF-1 compounds often discussed alongside TRT — follistatin-344, ACE-031, PEG-MGF, IGF-1 LR3, and IGF-1 DES — are research-only and are not approved.
