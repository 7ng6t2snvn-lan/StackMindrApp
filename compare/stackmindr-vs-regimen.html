StackMindr vs Regimen

Two of the most-discussed trackers for peptides, GLP-1s, TRT and HRT. They overlap on the basics — schedules, reminders, labs — but they make different bets. Here's an honest side-by-side so you can pick the one that fits how you actually run your protocol.

The short answer

Both StackMindr and Regimen are capable, cross-platform (iOS and Android) trackers covering peptides, GLP-1s, testosterone and more. If you want the quick version: StackMindr leans into planning and everyday usefulness — a supply-and-budget planner that tells you what a cycle actually costs, Home and Lock Screen widgets, PDF lab import with marker trends, a conversational AI assistant, and a more generous free tier. Regimen leans into modeling — pharmacokinetic blood-level estimates across a large compound library and photo-based lab scanning.

Neither is "better" for everyone. It comes down to whether you value cost planning, glanceable widgets and a chat-style assistant, or deep level-curve modeling across many compounds.

Where StackMindr leads

  • Budget & cost planning. StackMindr's Supply & Budget Planner tells you how many vials you'll need, when a cycle runs, and what it costs — broken down per cycle, per month, per week, and per dose. Regimen doesn't have a dedicated cost planner, so this is a genuine gap StackMindr fills.
  • Home & Lock Screen widgets. Your next dose and how many you have left today, right on your home screen — no app-opening required. This is a StackMindr-only feature in this matchup.
  • PDF lab import with trends. Upload a lab-report PDF and StackMindr extracts your markers, then charts each one over time against your own reference ranges. It shows your data — it doesn't interpret or flag it.
  • A real conversational assistant. StackMindr's AI is chat-style — ask about your protocol, timing, or stacking, or get a "doctor-visit summary" you can bring to an appointment. Regimen's insights are automated pattern flags rather than a back-and-forth assistant.
  • A more generous free tier. Track up to three compounds free before any paywall, versus roughly one on Regimen.

Where Regimen leads

In the interest of an honest comparison, Regimen does several things well:

  • Pharmacokinetic level modeling. Regimen models estimated blood levels — peak, trough and steady state — across a large library of compounds. If seeing projected level curves for many different compounds is your priority, that depth is a real strength.
  • Photo lab scanning. You can photograph a lab panel and have it read, which some people find quicker than exporting a PDF.
  • Broader Health Connect metrics. Regimen syncs a wide set of health metrics; StackMindr currently focuses on sleep for its health integration.
  • A larger stated user base and compound library. More compounds and brands listed, and a larger community of users at the time of writing.

Which one is right for you?

Here's the honest framing:

  • Lean StackMindr if you want to know what your protocol costs, like checking your next dose from a widget, prefer uploading lab PDFs and charting your own results, want a chat-style assistant and a doctor-visit summary, or just want more room on the free tier.
  • Lean Regimen if your top priority is projected blood-level curves across a wide range of compounds, you prefer photographing labs, or you want the broadest set of synced health metrics.

Both are free to try, so the low-risk move is to install the one that matches your priority above and see which fits your routine. You enter your own protocol in either app — neither recommends a dose, and neither is a substitute for your prescriber.

StackMindr vs Regimen FAQ

Is StackMindr or Regimen better?

It depends on what you value. StackMindr leads on budget and cost planning, Home and Lock Screen widgets, PDF lab import with marker trends, a conversational AI assistant and doctor-visit summary, and a more generous free tier (up to three compounds versus one). Regimen leads on pharmacokinetic blood-level modeling across a large library and photo-based lab scanning. Both track peptides, GLP-1s, TRT and HRT on iOS and Android.

How much do StackMindr and Regimen cost?

Both offer a free version and a paid subscription. StackMindr Pro is around $4.99/month; Regimen's premium is around $39.99/year. Pricing changes over time, so check the App Store or Google Play for current figures.

Which app has a better free version?

StackMindr's free tier lets you track up to three compounds with schedules, reminders, inventory and injection-site tracking. Regimen's free tier is generally limited to one compound. If you run more than one compound, StackMindr goes further before any paywall.

Does StackMindr track GLP-1, TRT and peptides like Regimen?

Yes. StackMindr covers peptides, GLP-1s (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide and more), testosterone and other hormones, hCG, and supplements — with schedules, reminders, inventory, and a supply and budget planner for any of them.

Can I import my blood work into StackMindr?

Yes. StackMindr Pro lets you upload a lab-report PDF; it extracts your markers and charts each one over time against your own reference ranges in the Labs tab. It displays your results — it does not interpret, diagnose, or flag them.

Does StackMindr have a home-screen widget?

Yes. StackMindr offers Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets showing your next dose and how many you have left today, so you can check your protocol at a glance without opening the app.
