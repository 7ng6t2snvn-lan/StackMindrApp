GHRP-2 vs GHRP-6

These two are close siblings — both GHRPs that trigger a growth-hormone pulse the same way. The practical difference comes down to one thing: how much they make you want to eat. GHRP-6's appetite spike is its signature; GHRP-2 brings strong release with less hunger.

The short answer

GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 are from the same family. Both are secretagogues (GHRPs) that mimic ghrelin and trigger a growth-hormone pulse through the same receptor, and both are effective, older-generation releasers. On the mechanism, they're genuinely similar — which is why the choice between them is rarely about raw strength.

GHRP-2 is frequently referenced as a strong GH releaser that comes with noticeably less hunger than GHRP-6, though it can carry a slight prolactin and cortisol effect. GHRP-6's defining feature is marked appetite stimulation — that's the thing it's best known for. So the honest decision usually isn't "which is better," it's whether you want the appetite stimulation or would rather avoid it.

How they work

GHRP-2 mimics ghrelin and binds the growth-hormone secretagogue receptor to prompt a GH pulse. It's commonly referenced as one of the stronger releasers in the GHRP family while producing less of the hunger that defines GHRP-6. It isn't fully selective, though — a slight prolactin and cortisol effect is part of its profile, which is typical of the older GHRPs.

GHRP-6 works through the same receptor and the same ghrelin-mimicking route, and it's an effective GH releaser too. The difference is that its ghrelin signaling spills strongly into appetite, producing the marked hunger it's famous for. Like GHRP-2, it's less selective than a modern GHRP such as ipamorelin, so a small prolactin and cortisol effect can come along with it.

The appetite question decides it

Because GH release is broadly comparable between the two, the deciding factor is almost always appetite. GHRP-6's hunger stimulation is pronounced and well known; for some people that's disruptive, for others it's exactly what they're after. GHRP-2 keeps the strong release but dials the hunger back, which is why it tends to get referenced when appetite stimulation is unwanted.

Neither of these is the "clean" option — that's ipamorelin's role. Between GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 specifically, both carry a slight prolactin and cortisol effect, so the meaningful split really is the appetite dimension. Frame the choice around whether you want to drive hunger or keep it low, and the decision usually makes itself.

Side effects & safety

For both, the most common reported issues are mild and local — injection-site redness, a brief flush or head-rush, some water retention. The standout difference is appetite: GHRP-6's hunger spike can be significant, while GHRP-2's is milder. Both can carry a slight prolactin and cortisol effect given their older, less-selective profiles.

Both GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 are research compounds and are not approved medicines, so neither is a supplement to self-direct casually. Growth-hormone pathways interact with blood sugar, fluid balance, and other systems, and appetite changes in particular can have knock-on effects worth tracking. Anyone considering either should review their specific situation with a qualified provider. Nothing here is medical advice or a dosing recommendation.

Which one is right for you?

This isn't a decision to settle from a web page, but here's the honest framing people and their providers tend to weigh:

  • Leaning GHRP-2 — when you want strong GH release but would rather keep hunger in check, accepting a slight prolactin and cortisol effect.
  • Leaning GHRP-6 — when marked appetite stimulation is something you actually want, or you don't mind it alongside strong release.
  • Want it cleaner than either? — a highly selective GHRP like ipamorelin is the usual reference point when minimizing appetite, prolactin, and cortisol is the goal.

GHRP-2 vs GHRP-6 FAQ

What is the difference between GHRP-2 and GHRP-6?

Both are GHRPs — secretagogues that mimic ghrelin and trigger a growth-hormone pulse through the same receptor family. GHRP-2 produces strong GH release with less hunger than GHRP-6, though it can carry a slight prolactin and cortisol effect. GHRP-6 is best known for marked appetite stimulation, which is its signature trait.

Which causes more hunger, GHRP-2 or GHRP-6?

GHRP-6 causes markedly more hunger — pronounced appetite stimulation is its defining feature. GHRP-2 still has some appetite effect but noticeably less, which is why it is often referenced when strong GH release is wanted without the big hunger spike.

Is GHRP-2 stronger than GHRP-6?

Both are effective at triggering growth-hormone release and are often described as comparable in that respect. GHRP-2 is frequently referenced as a strong releaser with less hunger, while GHRP-6's appetite stimulation is its standout trait. The practical choice is usually less about raw strength and more about whether appetite stimulation is wanted or not.

Do GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 raise prolactin or cortisol?

Both are older, less selective GHRPs than ipamorelin and can carry a slight prolactin and cortisol effect. This is generally described as mild, but it is one reason more selective options like ipamorelin exist. Anyone concerned should review their situation with a qualified provider.

Are GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 approved medicines?

No. Both GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 are research compounds and are not approved drugs. They should not be treated as over-the-counter supplements, and anyone considering them should review their situation with a qualified provider.
