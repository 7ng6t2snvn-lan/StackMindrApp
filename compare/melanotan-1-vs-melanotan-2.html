Melanocortins · Comparison

Melanotan 1 vs Melanotan 2

Both are melanocortin peptides that drive the skin to produce more melanin. One is narrow and selective; the other is broad-acting and brings extra effects — and extra side effects. Here's how Melanotan I and Melanotan II actually differ.

The short answer

Melanotan I (also called afamelanotide) is the more selective melanocortin receptor agonist. Its activity is narrower and more targeted, aimed mainly at tanning and photoprotection, and it is approved in some regions for a rare light-sensitivity condition. Melanotan II is less selective — it activates a broader set of melanocortin receptors, so on top of tanning it is also associated with libido and erectile effects. That breadth comes at a cost: Melanotan II is linked to more side effects.

Reduced to one line: Melanotan I is the cleaner, more focused peptide with a real medical approval behind one of its forms, while Melanotan II is broader and stronger on cosmetic and sexual effects but noisier on side effects. Melanotan II is not an approved medicine and is sold only as a research compound.

How they work

Melanotan I is a synthetic analog of α-MSH that acts fairly selectively on the melanocortin-1 receptor (MC1R) in skin cells, prompting them to produce more eumelanin — the pigment linked to tanning and to natural protection against UV damage. Its selectivity is the key feature: by concentrating activity on the pigment pathway, its approved form (afamelanotide, brand name Scenesse) is used for erythropoietic protoporphyria, a disorder where sunlight causes severe pain.

Melanotan II is also an α-MSH analog but binds a wider range of melanocortin receptors, including MC4R, which is involved in sexual arousal and appetite. That broader binding is why it's associated with tanning and libido effects — and why the approved sexual-health peptide PT-141 (bremelanotide) was developed from it. The same lack of selectivity that broadens its effects also broadens its side effects.

Effects & potency: the honest trade-off

If the only goal discussed in research is pigmentation, Melanotan I is the more targeted tool and is generally reported to have a cleaner side-effect profile. Melanotan II is often described as producing a stronger or faster cosmetic tanning response, and it adds the libido and erectile effects that Melanotan I largely doesn't — but it does so by hitting more receptors than you may want engaged.

That trade-off is the whole story here. Broader activity means more of everything, wanted and unwanted. Neither is approved for cosmetic tanning, and Melanotan II in particular carries a reputation for side effects that many people underestimate. Potency is not the same as a better choice.

Side effects & safety

Melanotan II is commonly associated with nausea, facial flushing, spontaneous erections, and the darkening of existing moles and freckles. Because it drives broad melanin activity, changes to moles and other skin lesions are a specific concern — any new or changing mole is something to have evaluated. Blood-pressure and appetite changes have also been reported.

Melanotan I shares the general mechanism, so nausea and flushing are possible, but its narrower selectivity tends to mean fewer of the broader effects. Its approved form has a studied safety profile for its medical indication; the injectable research peptide does not. For either compound, darkening skin without UV protection does not make skin immune to sun damage. None of this is medical advice.

Which one is right for you?

Neither is an approved cosmetic product, so the honest framing is about understanding the difference rather than endorsing use:

  • Melanotan I — the more selective, cleaner-profile peptide, and the one with an actual regulatory approval behind its afamelanotide form for a specific medical condition.
  • Melanotan II — broader-acting, often described as a stronger tanning and libido effect, but with notably more side effects and no approval anywhere.
  • Watch your skin either way — any darkening or change in moles is a reason to see a dermatologist, and pigment is not a substitute for sun protection.

Melanotan 1 vs Melanotan 2 FAQ

What is the difference between Melanotan 1 and Melanotan 2?

Melanotan I (afamelanotide) is a more selective melanocortin agonist used mainly for tanning and photoprotection. Melanotan II is less selective and activates a broader range of melanocortin receptors, so alongside tanning it's also associated with libido and erectile effects — but with more side effects. In short, Melanotan I is narrower and more targeted; Melanotan II is broader-acting.

Is Melanotan 1 or Melanotan 2 approved?

Melanotan I, as afamelanotide, is approved in some regions — including the EU and the US — under the brand name Scenesse for erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP), a rare light-sensitivity disorder. Melanotan II is not an approved drug anywhere and is sold only as a research compound. Neither is approved for cosmetic tanning.

Which melanotan is better for tanning?

Both stimulate melanin, but they trade off differently. Melanotan I is more selective and generally reported to have a cleaner side-effect profile. Melanotan II is often associated with a stronger or faster cosmetic tan, but it brings broader melanocortin activity and more frequent side effects such as nausea and mole darkening. Melanotan II is not an approved medicine.

Does Melanotan 2 affect libido?

Melanotan II is associated with libido and erectile effects because of its broader melanocortin activity — spontaneous erections are commonly reported. The approved peptide PT-141 (bremelanotide) was derived from Melanotan II specifically for sexual-health use. Melanotan I is more selective and isn't primarily associated with these effects.

What are the side effects of Melanotan 2?

Melanotan II is commonly associated with nausea, facial flushing, spontaneous erections, and darkening of existing moles and freckles. Because it drives broad melanin activity, changes to moles are a particular reason skin lesions are monitored. It's not an approved drug, and these effects come from research and user reports, not a regulated label.
