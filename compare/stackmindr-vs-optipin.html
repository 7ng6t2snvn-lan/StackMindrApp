StackMindr vs OptiPin

Both apps track TRT, peptides and GLP-1s and both model your protocol over time — but they make a big platform choice differently. StackMindr runs on iOS and Android; OptiPin is iPhone-only with a local-first privacy model. Here's an honest side-by-side.

The short answer

The first fork is simple: OptiPin is iOS-only, while StackMindr is on both iOS and Android. If you're on Android, StackMindr is the one that runs on your phone. On iPhone, the choice is about priorities. StackMindr adds a full supply-and-budget planner, Home and Lock Screen widgets, and a more generous free tier. OptiPin's calling card is a local-first, no-account privacy model paired with bloodwork-calibrated level modeling and a wearable-based recovery score.

Both are genuinely good at the fundamentals — schedules, logging, labs, and insights. This comes down to platform, cost planning, and whether an on-device-only model is a must-have for you.

Where StackMindr leads

  • It runs on Android. OptiPin is iPhone-only. If you're on Android — or you switch between an Android phone and other devices — StackMindr is the one that actually runs on your hardware.
  • Full budget & cost planning. StackMindr tells you the vials you'll need and the cost per cycle, month, week and dose. OptiPin offers a cost-per-dose figure, but not a full budget breakdown.
  • Widgets today. Home and Lock Screen widgets are live in StackMindr; OptiPin has described them as planned. If a glanceable next-dose widget matters now, StackMindr has it.
  • A more generous free tier. Up to three compounds free, versus roughly one.

Where OptiPin leads

OptiPin is a well-built app with real strengths, and an honest comparison should say so:

  • Local-first privacy. No account and data kept on-device by default is OptiPin's signature. If you want a tracker that never requires sign-in and keeps everything local, that's exactly what it's designed for.
  • Bloodwork-calibrated level modeling. OptiPin recalibrates its estimated level curves against your actual lab results rather than population averages — a sophisticated touch for people who live in that data.
  • Wearable-driven recovery score. A nightly recovery score built from HRV, sleep and resting heart rate via Apple Watch, Whoop, Oura or Garmin. StackMindr focuses its health integration on sleep.
  • Rich export. A dedicated Doctor Summary PDF plus JSON and CSV export.

Which one is right for you?

  • Lean StackMindr if you use Android, want real cost/budget planning, like checking your next dose from a widget, or want more room on the free tier.
  • Lean OptiPin if you're on iPhone and your top priorities are a no-account on-device privacy model, bloodwork-calibrated level curves, or a wearable-based recovery score.

Both are free to start, and in both you enter your own protocol — neither recommends a dose or replaces your prescriber. Install the one that matches your top priority and see which fits your routine.

StackMindr vs OptiPin FAQ

Is StackMindr or OptiPin better?

They suit different people. StackMindr is on both iOS and Android, has a full supply-and-budget planner, Home and Lock Screen widgets, and a more generous free tier. OptiPin is iOS-only and focuses on an on-device, no-account privacy model with bloodwork-calibrated level modeling and a wearable-based recovery score. If you use Android, or want cost planning and widgets, StackMindr fits; if you want a local-first model and deep level modeling on iPhone, OptiPin is strong.

Does OptiPin work on Android?

No. OptiPin is iOS-only at the time of writing. StackMindr is available on both iOS and Android, so if you use an Android phone, StackMindr is the option that runs on your device.

How much do StackMindr and OptiPin cost?

Both have a free tier and a paid subscription in a similar range — StackMindr Pro is around $4.99/month, and OptiPin's premium is around $4.99/month or $39.99/year. Pricing changes, so check the App Store for current figures.

Which app has home-screen widgets?

StackMindr has Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets today. OptiPin has described widgets as planned but not yet shipped at the time of writing, so if a glanceable widget matters now, StackMindr is the one that has it.

Does StackMindr have a budget planner?

Yes. StackMindr's Supply & Budget Planner shows the vials you'll need and the cost per cycle, month, week and dose. OptiPin offers a cost-per-dose calculation but not a full budget breakdown, so StackMindr goes deeper on cost planning.

Which app is more private?

OptiPin's selling point is a local-first, no-account model where data stays on the device by default. StackMindr uses an account so your data syncs across devices, stores it securely, never sells it, and keeps health data like sleep on-device. If a no-account, on-device-only model is your top priority, OptiPin is built around that; if you want cross-device sync with strong privacy, StackMindr fits.
