CJC-1295 vs Ipamorelin

This is the most-referenced growth-hormone stack — and the "vs" is a little misleading. CJC-1295 and ipamorelin aren't really rivals; they're run together because they work on two different pathways that reinforce each other. Here's what each one actually contributes.

The short answer

Searching "CJC-1295 vs ipamorelin" usually turns up the same realization: these two aren't competitors, they're partners. CJC-1295 (no DAC, also called Mod GRF 1-29) is a short-acting GHRH analog that raises the signal to release growth hormone. Ipamorelin is a GHRP secretagogue that mimics ghrelin and triggers a growth-hormone pulse through a separate receptor. One raises the drive; the other fires the pulse.

Because the two mechanisms are distinct and complementary, they are almost always referenced together as the classic GH peptide stack. So the honest version of "vs" is: what does each bring to the pairing? CJC-1295 sets the stage by lifting the release signal; ipamorelin delivers a clean, selective pulse on top of it. The combined response is reported to be greater than either compound produces alone.

How they work

CJC-1295 without DAC is a modified fragment of GHRH — the natural hormone that tells the pituitary to release growth hormone. The "no DAC" form is short-acting, which matters: it raises the release signal in a relatively brief window rather than keeping it elevated for days the way the DAC version does. That short window is what lets it sync with a GHRP's pulse.

Ipamorelin works through a completely different receptor. It mimics ghrelin and binds the growth-hormone secretagogue receptor to trigger a distinct GH pulse. Its defining feature is selectivity — it's associated with little to no rise in cortisol, prolactin, or appetite, which is why it's the "clean" GHRP of choice in this pairing. GHRH signal plus GHRP pulse is the mechanism behind the stack.

Why they're a stack, not a choice

The reason this pairing is so frequently referenced is simple: the two pathways reinforce each other. A GHRH analog raises how much growth hormone is available to be released, and a GHRP triggers the release. Referenced together, the combined response is reported to exceed what either produces on its own — which is exactly why "CJC-1295 and ipamorelin" is shorthand for the classic GH stack rather than a genuine either/or.

If you're forcing a distinction anyway, it's this: CJC-1295 is the signal-raiser and ipamorelin is the pulse-trigger. Remove either and you lose half the logic of the pairing. The short-acting (no DAC) form of CJC-1295 is the one usually referenced here precisely because its timing lines up with the ipamorelin pulse.

Side effects & safety

Both are generally reported to be well tolerated in the contexts they've been studied, with the most common issues being mild and local — injection-site redness, a brief flush, or some water retention. Ipamorelin's selectivity is a frequent talking point: it's associated with far less cortisol, prolactin, or hunger response than older GHRPs, which is part of why it's paired with CJC-1295 so often.

Both CJC-1295 (no DAC) and ipamorelin are research compounds and are not approved medicines. That's an important line: neither is a supplement to self-direct casually. Growth-hormone pathways touch blood sugar, fluid balance, and other systems, so anyone considering the stack should review their specific situation with a qualified provider. Nothing here is medical advice or a dosing recommendation.

Which one is right for you?

With this pair, the honest framing isn't "pick one" — it's understanding what each does so the stack makes sense:

  • CJC-1295 (no DAC) — the GHRH-analog half; it raises the release signal and sets up the pulse. Short-acting so its timing matches the GHRP.
  • Ipamorelin — the GHRP half; it triggers a clean, selective pulse with minimal off-target effects.
  • Usually together — because the two mechanisms are synergistic, they're most often referenced as a single stack rather than as alternatives.
