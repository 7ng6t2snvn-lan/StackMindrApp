Ipamorelin vs GHRP-6

Both are GHRPs from the same family — they trigger a growth-hormone pulse the same way. The real split is selectivity: ipamorelin is the clean, modern option, while older GHRP-6 drives strong GH release but comes with a famously big appetite spike.

The short answer

Ipamorelin and GHRP-6 are cousins. Both are secretagogues (GHRPs) that mimic ghrelin and trigger a growth-hormone pulse through the same receptor family, so at the mechanism level they're doing the same thing. The difference people actually care about is how clean the signal is.

Ipamorelin is highly selective — it's associated with little to no rise in appetite, cortisol, or prolactin, which is why it's the modern default GHRP. GHRP-6 is the older compound; it produces strong GH release but is less selective, and its signature is pronounced appetite stimulation, with some potential to nudge cortisol and prolactin. If hunger is unwelcome, that difference decides it; if you specifically want appetite drive, it's the reason GHRP-6 still gets referenced.

How they work

Ipamorelin mimics ghrelin and binds the growth-hormone secretagogue receptor to trigger a GH pulse. Its defining trait is precision: it's engineered to be selective for growth-hormone release, so it's associated with very little spillover into appetite, cortisol, or prolactin. That clean profile is why it became the go-to GHRP in modern references.

GHRP-6 works through the same receptor and the same ghrelin-mimicking route, and it's effective at driving GH release. But as an earlier-generation compound it's less selective. Its strong ghrelin signaling is also what produces its hallmark effect — marked hunger — and that same lack of selectivity is why it's more often associated with small rises in cortisol and prolactin than ipamorelin is.

Selectivity & the appetite question

This is where the two genuinely diverge. GHRP-6's appetite stimulation isn't a minor footnote — it's the thing it's best known for. For someone who finds strong hunger disruptive, that alone is reason enough to reference ipamorelin instead. For someone who actually wants appetite drive, the same trait is a feature, and GHRP-6's older track record still gets it referenced.

Beyond hunger, selectivity matters because the off-target signals (cortisol and prolactin) are generally things people prefer to keep flat. Ipamorelin's appeal is that it delivers a GH pulse while leaving those alone as much as possible. GHRP-6 trades some of that cleanliness for its robust, old-school GH release. Neither is "wrong" — they're tuned differently.

Side effects & safety

For both, the most common reported issues are mild and local — injection-site redness, a brief flush or head-rush, some water retention. The headline difference is GHRP-6's appetite spike, which can be significant, plus its greater tendency to nudge cortisol and prolactin. Ipamorelin's selectivity is precisely what keeps those effects minimal.

Both ipamorelin and GHRP-6 are research compounds and are not approved medicines, so neither is a supplement to self-direct casually. Growth-hormone pathways interact with blood sugar, fluid balance, and other systems, and appetite changes in particular can have knock-on effects worth tracking. Anyone considering either should review their specific situation with a qualified provider. Nothing here is medical advice or a dosing recommendation.

Which one is right for you?

This isn't a decision to settle from a web page, but here's the honest framing people and their providers tend to weigh:

  • Leaning ipamorelin — when you want a clean, selective GH pulse and would rather not deal with a big appetite spike or off-target cortisol and prolactin.
  • Leaning GHRP-6 — when strong, old-school GH release is the goal, or when appetite stimulation is actually something you want rather than avoid.
  • Either as the GHRP half of a stack — a GHRP is often referenced alongside a GHRH analog; ipamorelin is the more common pick there for its selectivity.

Ipamorelin vs GHRP-6 FAQ

What is the difference between ipamorelin and GHRP-6?

Both are GHRPs — secretagogues that mimic ghrelin and trigger a growth-hormone pulse through the same receptor family. The difference is selectivity. Ipamorelin is highly selective, with minimal effect on appetite, cortisol, or prolactin. GHRP-6 is older and produces strong GH release but is associated with pronounced appetite stimulation and less selectivity, so it can nudge cortisol and prolactin.

Which is cleaner, ipamorelin or GHRP-6?

Ipamorelin is generally referred to as the cleaner of the two. It is highly selective for growth-hormone release and is associated with little to no rise in appetite, cortisol, or prolactin. GHRP-6, while effective at triggering GH release, is less selective and is known for marked hunger as a side effect.

Does GHRP-6 make you hungry?

Pronounced appetite stimulation is GHRP-6's signature. Because it mimics ghrelin strongly and is less selective than ipamorelin, it is commonly associated with a marked increase in hunger shortly after use. For some that's unwanted; for others who want to drive appetite it can be a reason to reference GHRP-6 over a cleaner GHRP.

Are ipamorelin and GHRP-6 approved medicines?

No. Both ipamorelin and GHRP-6 are research compounds and are not approved drugs. They should not be treated as over-the-counter supplements, and anyone considering them should review their situation with a qualified provider.

Can you stack ipamorelin or GHRP-6 with a GHRH analog?

Both are GHRPs, and a GHRP is commonly referenced alongside a GHRH analog (such as CJC-1295 or sermorelin) because the two pathways reinforce each other. Ipamorelin is the more common choice for that pairing because of its selectivity. This is a reference comparison only, not a dosing recommendation.
