Healing & Recovery Peptides

The peptides people reach for after an injury, a hard training block, or gut trouble — BPC-157 and TB-500 at the center, with a handful of specialists around them. This guide explains what each one is researched for, how they're stacked, and how honest the evidence actually is.

What are healing & recovery peptides?

Healing and recovery peptides are compounds studied for their role in tissue repair, injury recovery, inflammation, and gut health. They're the group people look to for a nagging tendon, a slow-healing ligament, post-surgical recovery, or a gut that won't settle. The flagship of the category is BPC-157, usually discussed right alongside its classic partner, TB-500.

The category isn't one mechanism — it's a cluster of compounds that touch repair from different angles:

  • Systemic & localized repair — BPC-157 and TB-500, generally framed as the one that works near the injury and the one that works body-wide.
  • Skin, hair & collagen — GHK-Cu, a copper peptide associated with wound healing and collagen.
  • Inflammation — KPV and LL-37, short peptides studied for inflammatory and host-defense roles.
  • Gut barrier — larazotide, a tight-junction regulator studied in celiac disease.
  • Specialized pathways — ARA-290, B7-33, TP508, and AOD-9604, each tied to a specific target like neuropathy, fibrosis, angiogenesis, or cartilage.

One honest caveat up front: most of these are research compounds rather than approved medicines, and a lot of what's claimed for them rests on animal and preclinical data. The science is genuinely interesting, but it's early, and this guide is a reference — not a promise.

Flagship repair

BPC-157

A pentadecapeptide derived from a protein in gastric juice. Commonly referenced for localized repair, angiogenesis, and gut health — the category's flagship.

 Systemic repair

TB-500

A synthetic thymosin beta-4 fragment described as acting systemically — cell migration and flexibility. BPC-157's classic partner.

 Copper peptide

GHK-Cu

A copper-binding peptide associated with skin, hair, collagen, and wound healing.

 Anti-inflammatory

KPV

A short peptide studied for its anti-inflammatory effects in the gut and skin.

 Host defense

LL-37

An antimicrobial, host-defense peptide studied for its role in immunity and wound environments.

 HGH fragment

AOD-9604

A modified HGH fragment studied for fat loss, and also explored for cartilage and healing.

 Gut barrier

Larazotide

A gut-barrier and tight-junction regulator, studied in human trials for celiac disease.

 EPO-derived

ARA-290

A small peptide derived from erythropoietin (EPO), studied for neuropathy and inflammation.

 Relaxin analog

B7-33

A relaxin analog studied for anti-fibrotic effects and tissue remodeling.

 Tissue repair

TP508 (Chrysalin)

A peptide studied for angiogenesis and tissue and bone repair.

 Active fragment

Thymosin Beta-4 Fragment

The active fragment of thymosin beta-4 that underlies the actions attributed to TB-500.

 Joint / cartilage

Pentosan Polysulfate

A sulfated polysaccharide (not a peptide) used off-label for joint and cartilage support; oral Elmiron is approved for bladder pain.

Healing & Recovery Peptides FAQ

What are healing and recovery peptides?

Compounds studied for their role in tissue repair, injury recovery, inflammation, and gut health. The best-known are BPC-157 and TB-500, frequently discussed for tendon, ligament, and soft-tissue repair. Others target skin and collagen (GHK-Cu), gut and skin inflammation (KPV), or specific pathways like angiogenesis and tight-junction regulation. Most are research compounds supported largely by animal and preclinical data rather than approved human treatments.

What is the difference between BPC-157 and TB-500?

BPC-157 is a pentadecapeptide derived from a protein in gastric juice and is commonly referenced for localized repair, angiogenesis, and gut health. TB-500 is a synthetic fragment related to thymosin beta-4 and is described as acting more systemically, supporting cell migration and flexibility. Because one is framed as local and the other as systemic, they're the classic pairing — sometimes sold together as a "wolverine" healing stack.

Are healing peptides FDA approved?

Most are not. BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu, KPV, LL-37, AOD-9604, ARA-290, B7-33, and TP508 are research compounds without approval as general healing or recovery treatments, and much of the evidence for them is animal or preclinical. Larazotide has been studied in human trials for celiac disease but is not an approved drug. None of these is a substitute for medical care.

Can healing peptides be stacked together?

The most commonly referenced combination is BPC-157 with TB-500 — the idea being that one is framed as more localized and the other as more systemic, so they're used together for injury recovery. GHK-Cu is sometimes added for skin, hair, and collagen support. Any combination should be discussed with a knowledgeable provider; stacking is not a dosing recommendation.
