What is Melanotan II?

Melanotan II (often shortened to MT-2 or MT-II) is a synthetic peptide that mimics alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH), a natural hormone that tells pigment cells in your skin to make more melanin. It was first developed by researchers at the University of Arizona who were exploring whether a "tan without the sun" could reduce the UV damage that leads to skin cancer.

What makes MT-2 distinct from the natural hormone is its breadth. It is a non-selective agonist, meaning it activates several melanocortin receptors rather than just one. Activating MC1R drives pigmentation, while activating MC4R in the brain influences sexual arousal and appetite — which is why the same peptide is talked about for two very different reasons: tanning and libido.

It is important to be clear about regulatory status. Melanotan II is not an approved medicine anywhere. It is not the same thing as afamelanotide (Scenesse), a related but distinct, approved melanocortin drug used for a rare light-sensitivity disorder. MT-2 is sold only as a "research chemical" of unverified purity, and health regulators in several countries have specifically warned against unlicensed Melanotan products.

How Melanotan II is thought to work

Melanotan II's effects trace back to the melanocortin receptor family. By binding these receptors more potently and for longer than the body's own α-MSH, it produces outsized downstream effects:

  • MC1R → pigmentation — activating melanocortin-1 receptors on melanocytes ramps up production of eumelanin, the darker pigment, which is the basis of the tanning effect. Sun or UV exposure is often reported to amplify and "set" the color.
  • MC4R → sexual arousal — activating melanocortin-4 receptors in the central nervous system is linked to increased sexual desire and spontaneous erections, independent of the vascular mechanism that drugs like sildenafil use.
  • MC4R → appetite suppression — the same central pathway is involved in satiety signaling, which is why reduced appetite is a commonly reported effect.
  • Melanocyte stimulation — broad activation can also darken existing moles and freckles, which is relevant to the safety section below.

In short, one peptide reaches both skin and brain, which explains its mixed reputation as both a cosmetic and a sexual-health compound.

What the research shows

The melanocortin system itself is well characterized, and the closely related drug bremelanotide (an MC4R-focused peptide) is FDA-approved for a specific sexual-desire condition — which lends biological plausibility to MT-2's libido effects. Early academic studies on Melanotan II did report increased skin pigmentation and effects on erectile function in small groups.

But the honest summary is that MT-2 as it is sold today is experimental and under-studied in humans for the uses people seek. There are no large, modern, high-quality trials establishing that recreational dosing is safe or effective, and because the product is unregulated, what is actually in a given vial is uncertain. Treat dramatic online claims with skepticism. This page is educational and does not endorse use.

How Melanotan II is commonly used

In the communities that discuss it, MT-2 is almost always used by subcutaneous injection. A typical pattern described is a loading phase — starting very low (often around 100 mcg) and titrating upward to roughly 250-500 mcg while pigment develops — followed by an occasional maintenance dose once the desired color is reached. These are commonly referenced figures for reference only, not a protocol to follow.

Melanotan II arrives as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder that has to be reconstituted with bacteriostatic water before use. Commonly stacked with

People interested in MT-2 for sexual health sometimes reference PT-141 (bremelanotide), a related melanocortin peptide focused specifically on arousal without the strong tanning effect. Those interested in the pigmentation side sometimes compare it to Melanotan I (afamelanotide's research analog), which is more selective for skin and generally described as having a milder side-effect profile.

Side effects & safety considerations

Melanotan II is one of the more side-effect-heavy compounds people discuss. Commonly reported effects include nausea (especially in the first days and right after a dose), facial flushing, reduced appetite, yawning, and spontaneous erections. Many of these are dose-dependent, which is part of why the "start low" advice is universal.

The more serious concern is the skin itself. Because MT-2 stimulates melanocytes broadly, it commonly darkens existing moles and freckles and can prompt new ones. Any new, changing, or asymmetric mole is a reason to stop and see a dermatologist, because melanoma is a real risk and a pigmentation agent that is unregulated and unstudied long-term offers no reassurance here. Importantly, a MT-2 tan does not make skin UV-proof and does not prevent skin cancer.

Everything above is educational, not medical advice. Melanotan II is not approved, purity is unverified, and there is no long-term human safety record. Anyone considering it should review it — and their skin history — with a qualified healthcare provider first.

Melanotan II FAQ

What is a common Melanotan II dose?

Commonly referenced protocols start low — around 100 mcg — and titrate up over a loading phase toward roughly 250-500 mcg, then drop to an occasional maintenance dose once the desired pigmentation is reached. This is educational reference only; Melanotan II is not approved, and dosing should be discussed with a qualified healthcare provider.

How do you reconstitute Melanotan II?

Melanotan II is supplied as a lyophilized powder and mixed with bacteriostatic water before subcutaneous injection. StackMindr's free reconstitution calculator gives you the exact units to draw for your vial size and target dose on a U-100 insulin syringe.

Is Melanotan II approved or legal?

No. Melanotan II is not approved by the FDA or comparable regulators for any use, and it is not the same as approved afamelanotide. It is sold only as a research chemical of unverified purity. Regulators in several countries have issued warnings about unlicensed Melanotan products.

Does Melanotan II actually cause tanning?

It stimulates melanocortin-1 receptors, which increase melanin production, so users commonly report darker skin, often with less sun exposure than usual. It does not make skin UV-proof and does not prevent skin cancer. Any pigment change is an experimental cosmetic effect, not a sun-protection strategy.

What are the side effects of Melanotan II?

Commonly reported effects include nausea (especially early on), facial flushing, reduced appetite, spontaneous erections, and darkening of moles and freckles. New or changing moles are a reason to stop and see a dermatologist, because melanoma risk is a real concern with an unregulated pigmentation agent.
