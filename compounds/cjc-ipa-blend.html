What is the CJC / Ipamorelin blend?

The CJC / Ipamorelin blend combines two different kinds of growth-hormone secretagogue — compounds that prompt your own pituitary to release growth hormone — into a single injection. It pairs CJC-1295 (no DAC), a short-acting GHRH analog (modified GRF 1-29), with Ipamorelin, a selective ghrelin-receptor agonist (a GHRP).

The reason this particular pairing is the most popular GH stack is that the two peptides approach the pituitary through completely different doors, and their effects are complementary. Together they produce a larger, cleaner growth-hormone pulse than either peptide produces alone. Neither component is an FDA-approved drug, and the blend is used in performance, recovery and anti-aging contexts rather than as an approved therapy.

How the blend is thought to work

The whole rationale rests on combining two mechanisms that reinforce each other:

  • CJC-1295 (GHRH analog) — binds the GHRH receptor and increases the amount of growth hormone released in each pulse.
  • Ipamorelin (GHRP) — binds the separate ghrelin/GH-secretagogue receptor, triggering a GH pulse and suppressing somatostatin, the hormone that normally puts the brakes on GH release.
  • Synergy — one pushes harder on the accelerator while the other releases the brake, so the combined GH pulse is larger than simply adding the two effects.
  • Selectivity of Ipamorelin — unlike older GHRPs, ipamorelin is relatively selective, meaning it is reported to raise GH with minimal effect on cortisol or prolactin, which is why it is the preferred GHRP partner.

Crucially, because the blend still works through your own pituitary, it preserves the body’s natural pulsing rhythm rather than flooding the system with constant growth hormone.

What the research shows

The two building blocks are individually studied, and the underlying principle — that combining a GHRH analog with a GHRP produces a greater GH release than either alone — is a well-established finding in endocrinology. Foundational work on modified GRF/CJC analogs demonstrated meaningful, sustained increases in GH and IGF-1, and ipamorelin’s selectivity is well documented.

What is thinner is formal clinical study of the specific branded blend for the body-composition, recovery and anti-aging outcomes people seek, and long-term human safety data at the doses used recreationally. The mechanism is sound and widely accepted; the gap is in rigorous outcome trials of this exact use. The honest summary is “well-founded mechanism and solid component pharmacology, limited formal evidence for the popular long-term use.”

How the blend is commonly used

A commonly referenced configuration is around 100 mcg of each component, once daily, typically at bedtime and on an empty stomach — timing chosen to align with the body’s largest natural GH pulse and to avoid food (especially carbohydrate and fat) blunting the response. Some users dose more than once daily.

Injectable the CJC / Ipamorelin blend arrives as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder that has to be reconstituted with bacteriostatic water before use. Commonly stacked with

This blend is itself a stack of its two components — CJC-1295 (no DAC) and Ipamorelin — which are also used individually. It sits alongside other GH-axis peptides such as CJC-1293, Sermorelin (an older GHRH analog) and the FDA-approved Tesamorelin. Side effects & safety considerations

Reported side effects track with raising growth hormone and IGF-1: injection-site reactions, water retention, tingling or numbness in the hands, transient flushing, and occasionally headache or lightheadedness. Ipamorelin’s selectivity is meant to keep cortisol and prolactin largely unaffected, which is part of its appeal as the GHRP partner.

Because the blend increases growth signaling, the standard GH cautions apply — effects on blood sugar and insulin sensitivity, and particular caution for anyone with a history of cancer or active tumors, since GH/IGF-1 can influence cell growth. Neither component is approved, long-term human safety data is limited, and research-supplier purity and the accuracy of the blend ratio vary. CJC / Ipamorelin Blend FAQ

What is the CJC / Ipamorelin blend?

It is a combination of CJC-1295 (no DAC), a GHRH analog, and Ipamorelin, a selective GHRP, in one injection. It is the most popular growth-hormone secretagogue stack, used to prompt the body’s own GH release.

Why combine CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin?

They act on two different receptors. CJC-1295 increases the amount of GH released per pulse, while Ipamorelin triggers a pulse and suppresses somatostatin — so together they produce a larger GH pulse than either alone.

Does the blend raise cortisol or prolactin?

Ipamorelin is relatively selective and is reported to raise GH with minimal effect on cortisol or prolactin, which is why it is the preferred GHRP for this blend compared with older GHRPs.

Is the CJC / Ipamorelin blend FDA approved?

No. Neither component is an FDA-approved drug. The blend is used off-label and in research and enhancement contexts, not as an approved therapy.

When is the blend usually taken?

A commonly referenced setup is about 100 mcg of each component once daily at bedtime on an empty stomach, to align with the body’s natural nighttime GH pulse and avoid food blunting the response.

What are the common side effects?

Injection-site reactions, water retention, tingling in the hands, flushing and occasional headache — effects associated with raising GH and IGF-1. Standard GH cautions apply, including for anyone with a cancer history.
