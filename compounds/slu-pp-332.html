What is SLU-PP-332?

SLU-PP-332 is a synthetic small molecule — not a peptide — developed as an agonist (activator) of the estrogen-related receptors, a family of nuclear receptors (ERRα, ERRβ, and ERRγ) that help control how cells produce and use energy. It came out of academic drug-discovery work and has been nicknamed an “exercise mimetic” because it appears to switch on some of the same cellular programs that endurance exercise does.

The excitement is easy to understand: the idea of a compound that delivers endurance-and-fat-loss adaptations without the training is appealing. In mouse studies it produced measurable gains in running capacity and reductions in fat mass.

Accuracy matters: SLU-PP-332 is research-stage. The findings are from mice, there are no human clinical trials, and it is not an approved drug or an established supplement. It is included here for education, not as something with a validated human use.

How SLU-PP-332 is thought to work

The proposed mechanism centers on the ERR family and cellular energy metabolism. Effects described in the research literature include:

  • ERR activation — directly binding and activating the estrogen-related receptors, which are master regulators of oxidative metabolism genes.
  • Mitochondrial biogenesis — upregulating the creation of new mitochondria, the cell's energy factories, a key adaptation to endurance training.
  • Increased fat oxidation — shifting metabolism toward burning fat for fuel in the models studied.
  • Endurance gene programs — switching on patterns of gene expression that overlap with those triggered by aerobic exercise, hence the “exercise mimetic” label.

These mechanisms are characterized in cells and rodents. Whether they produce meaningful, safe effects in humans is entirely unknown.

What the research shows

The headline studies (from around 2023 onward) are in mice, where SLU-PP-332 increased running endurance, boosted markers of oxidative metabolism, and reduced fat mass without changes in food intake. As preclinical metabolic science, that is a striking result and the reason the compound got attention.

But there are no human trials, no human dosing, and no human safety record. Its rapid jump from a 2023 research finding to a product sold online is a case study in how quickly a promising preclinical molecule can outrun its own evidence. The honest summary is “impressive in mice, completely untested in people.” Claims that it works as a fat-loss or endurance product in humans are ahead of the evidence.

How SLU-PP-332 is studied

In the published research, SLU-PP-332 has been administered by injection (intraperitoneal) to mice. The 10-50 mg figures that circulate are extrapolations and research references, not a validated human dose — there is no established human route or schedule.

One practical point worth stating clearly: because SLU-PP-332 is a small molecule rather than a lyophilized peptide, the reconstitution-and-insulin-syringe workflow used for injectable peptides does not apply to it, and StackMindr's reconstitution calculator is not the right tool here. It is best understood as an early research compound rather than something with a worked-out usage protocol.

There is also an important translation gap to keep in mind. Mice and humans differ substantially in metabolism and in how they respond to drugs that act on nuclear receptors, so a dramatic endurance or fat-loss effect in a mouse does not reliably predict the same effect — or the same safety — in a person. The doses used in rodent studies cannot simply be scaled down to a capsule, and the long-term consequences of chronically activating ERR signaling in humans are unknown. These are exactly the questions that formal clinical trials exist to answer, and for SLU-PP-332 those trials have not happened.

Side effects & safety considerations

Because SLU-PP-332 has never been tested in humans, there is no human safety data — no characterized side effects, no understood dosing, and no knowledge of long-term effects. Compounds that broadly reprogram metabolism through nuclear receptors can have wide-ranging effects, which is exactly why they go through years of safety testing before any human use.

Identity and purity from unregulated suppliers are unverified. This is a compound to follow in the research literature, not to experiment with. None of this is medical advice: anyone curious about it should discuss it with a qualified healthcare provider.

SLU-PP-332 FAQ

What is SLU-PP-332?

A synthetic small-molecule agonist of the estrogen-related receptors (ERRs), studied as an “exercise mimetic.” It is not a peptide.

What does SLU-PP-332 do?

In mice it activates ERRs to increase mitochondrial biogenesis, fat oxidation, and endurance, mimicking some adaptations of aerobic exercise. These results are preclinical only.

Is there human data on SLU-PP-332?

No. All published studies are in mice. There are no human clinical trials, no validated human dose, and no human safety record.

Do you reconstitute SLU-PP-332?

No. It is a small molecule, not a lyophilized peptide, so the peptide reconstitution-and-insulin-syringe workflow and StackMindr's reconstitution calculator do not apply.

Is SLU-PP-332 a safe fat-loss supplement?

It is not a validated supplement. There is no human safety data, and claims of fat-loss or endurance benefits in people are ahead of the evidence.

Is SLU-PP-332 FDA approved?

No. SLU-PP-332 is an early-stage research compound with no approved use and no human trials.
