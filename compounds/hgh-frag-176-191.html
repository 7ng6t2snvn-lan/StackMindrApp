HGH Frag 176-191

A small fragment taken from the tail end of the growth hormone molecule, studied for its effects on fat metabolism — without the growth-promoting or IGF-1 effects of full growth hormone.

What is HGH Frag 176-191?

HGH Frag 176-191 is a synthetic peptide that reproduces a short section near the end of the human growth hormone (HGH) molecule — specifically the amino acids at positions 176 through 191. Researchers isolated this region because it appears to carry much of growth hormone's effect on fat metabolism while leaving behind the parts responsible for general tissue growth.

That separation is the entire point of the fragment. Full growth hormone raises IGF-1, can promote growth across many tissues, and is associated with effects on blood sugar and insulin sensitivity. The 176-191 fragment is studied specifically as a lipolytic (fat-releasing) agent that, in the research literature, does not meaningfully move IGF-1 or produce the broad growth signaling of the parent hormone.

It is worth being clear up front: HGH Frag 176-191 is not an approved medicine, and most of what is known about it comes from laboratory and animal work rather than large human trials. It is discussed widely in body-composition circles, but it is not established as a treatment for obesity or any other condition.

How HGH Frag 176-191 is thought to work

The mechanisms described in the research center on fat cells rather than on growth signaling. The effects most often attributed to the fragment include:

  • Stimulating lipolysis — encouraging fat cells to break down stored triglycerides and release fatty acids, particularly in adipose (fat) tissue.
  • Inhibiting lipogenesis — reducing the formation and storage of new fat, as reported in some animal models.
  • Sparing IGF-1 and glucose pathways — unlike full growth hormone, the fragment is described as having little effect on IGF-1 levels or on insulin sensitivity in the available studies.

Because the fragment is thought to act mainly on fat metabolism, it is usually framed as a body-composition tool rather than a growth or recovery compound. How reliably these mechanisms translate from the lab bench to real-world human fat loss is not well established.

What the research shows

Early work on the 176-191 region in animal and in-vitro models reported increased fat breakdown and reduced fat accumulation, which is what first generated interest in it as a weight-management candidate. The closely related modified analog AOD-9604 was later developed from this same region and advanced into human obesity trials, where its weight-loss results were modest and did not clear the bar for approval.

For HGH Frag 176-191 itself, high-quality human data is limited. Much of what circulates online is anecdotal reports of accelerated fat loss. The honest summary is that the fat-metabolism mechanism is biologically plausible and supported in preclinical work, but robust evidence that it produces significant fat loss in people is lacking. Treat dramatic before-and-after claims with skepticism.

How HGH Frag 176-191 is commonly used

Commonly referenced protocols use 250–500 mcg per dose, typically once daily, often administered subcutaneously on an empty stomach because the presence of food (and the insulin response to it) is thought to blunt the fat-mobilizing effect. Some people split the dose or time it around fasted training.

HGH Frag 176-191 is supplied as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder that must be reconstituted with bacteriostatic water before injection. Commonly stacked with

HGH Frag 176-191 is most often discussed alongside other fat-loss and growth-hormone-adjacent compounds. AOD-9604 is its closest relative — a stabilized analog of the same region — so the two are rarely run together but are frequently compared. Some people pair it with a growth-hormone secretagogue such as those in the CJC/ipamorelin family for a broader body-composition approach, and the mitochondrial peptide MOTS-c is sometimes mentioned in the same metabolic context.

Side effects & safety considerations

In anecdotal use and the limited research available, HGH Frag 176-191 is generally described as well tolerated, with injection-site reactions (redness, mild irritation) being the most common complaint. Because the fragment is reported to leave IGF-1 and blood sugar largely untouched, it is often positioned as having a cleaner profile than full growth hormone — but that comparison rests on thin human data.

The real limitation is that there is no long-term human safety record, and product purity varies widely across suppliers of a compound that is not regulated as a medicine. None of this is medical advice. Anyone considering HGH Frag 176-191 should review it with a qualified healthcare provider who knows their history.

HGH Frag 176-191 FAQ

What is a common HGH Frag 176-191 dose?

Commonly referenced protocols use 250–500 mcg once daily, often injected on an empty stomach. This is educational reference only — dosing should be set with a qualified healthcare provider.

Does HGH Frag 176-191 raise IGF-1 like growth hormone?

No. The fragment is studied specifically because it targets fat metabolism without meaningfully raising IGF-1 or producing the broad growth signaling of full growth hormone, at least in the available research.

How is HGH Frag 176-191 different from AOD-9604?

AOD-9604 is a modified, stabilized analog developed from the same 176-191 region of growth hormone. They share the same fat-metabolism rationale, and AOD-9604 is the version that was actually taken into human obesity trials.

Is HGH Frag 176-191 FDA approved?

No. HGH Frag 176-191 is not an approved drug, and its evidence base is largely preclinical. It is not a treatment for obesity or any condition.

Does it work better fasted?

It is commonly used on an empty stomach because food and the resulting insulin response are thought to blunt fat mobilization. This is a common-practice rationale, not an established clinical finding.
