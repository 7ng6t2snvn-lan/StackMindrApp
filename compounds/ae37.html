What is AE37?

AE37 is an investigational cancer vaccine, not a performance or recovery peptide. It is built from a fragment of the HER2/neu protein — a marker overexpressed on many breast and some prostate tumors — linked to an “Ii-Key” modifier that makes the immune system respond to it more strongly.

Unlike most tumor vaccines, which aim at killer (CD8+) T-cells, AE37 is engineered to recruit CD4+ helper T-cells. Helper cells orchestrate and sustain the broader immune response, and the hypothesis is that a strong helper response produces more durable anti-tumor immunity and immunologic memory. AE37 has only ever been used inside clinical trials; it is not available as an approved therapy.

How AE37 is thought to work

The mechanism is immunological rather than metabolic:

  • Antigen presentation — the HER2-derived peptide is displayed to the immune system as a target associated with cancer cells.
  • Ii-Key enhancement — the attached Ii-Key segment improves how efficiently the peptide is loaded onto MHC class II molecules, which are what helper T-cells read.
  • CD4+ helper activation — this drives a T-helper response specific to HER2, intended to support and broaden anti-tumor immunity.
  • Immunologic memory — the goal is a lasting response that can recognize HER2-expressing cells later, which is why it has been studied in the adjuvant setting to reduce recurrence rather than to shrink existing tumors.

Vaccines of this type are typically given as a series of inoculations, often with an immune adjuvant such as GM-CSF, rather than as an ongoing daily dose.

What the research shows

AE37 has been evaluated in human clinical trials, chiefly as an adjuvant breast cancer vaccine intended to prevent recurrence in patients after standard treatment, and also explored in prostate cancer. It reliably generated the intended HER2-specific immune response and was generally well tolerated.

On the key question — does it improve outcomes — results have been mixed and modest. Across the overall trial populations the benefit did not reach the thresholds that would establish it as a therapy, though exploratory analyses suggested possible benefit in specific subgroups, such as patients with lower HER2 expression or triple-negative disease. AE37 is one of several HER2-directed peptide vaccines explored in this era, and like its peers it is typically given together with an immune adjuvant (such as GM-CSF) to strengthen the response. It remains research-stage: promising as an immunologic concept, not established as an effective treatment.

How AE37 is commonly used

AE37 is used only within clinical-trial protocols, so there is no consumer dosing standard. Research figures reference roughly 500 mcg to 1 mg per inoculation, given on a scheduled series rather than daily, typically intradermally with an immune adjuvant.

Injectable AE37 arrives as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder that has to be reconstituted with bacteriostatic water before use. Side effects & safety considerations

In trials AE37 was generally well tolerated, with the most common complaints being local injection-site reactions (redness, swelling) and mild flu-like symptoms typical of an active immune response — effects partly driven by the adjuvant it is given with.

The larger point is context: AE37 is an experimental oncology agent whose entire rationale is treating or preventing cancer recurrence in a monitored clinical setting. It is not a general wellness or longevity peptide, and using it outside a trial means no medical oversight, unknown product quality, and no established benefit. This is not medical advice; any use should be under the care of a qualified oncology provider.

AE37 FAQ

What is AE37?

AE37 is an investigational cancer vaccine made from a HER2-derived peptide joined to an Ii-Key modifier. It is designed to stimulate CD4+ helper T-cells against HER2-expressing tumors and has only been used in clinical trials.

How does AE37 work?

The Ii-Key modifier improves how the HER2 peptide is presented on MHC class II molecules, driving a CD4+ helper T-cell response. The aim is durable, HER2-specific anti-tumor immunity rather than directly shrinking a tumor.

What cancers has AE37 been studied for?

Mainly breast cancer, in the adjuvant setting to help prevent recurrence after standard treatment, and also prostate cancer. It generated the intended immune response but showed only mixed, modest outcome benefits overall.

Is AE37 FDA approved?

No. AE37 is a research-stage immunotherapy evaluated in clinical trials and is not an approved treatment for any condition.

How is AE37 given?

As a series of scheduled inoculations, typically intradermal and combined with an immune adjuvant such as GM-CSF, rather than as a daily dose. All reported use is within trial protocols.
