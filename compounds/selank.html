What is Selank?

Selank is a synthetic peptide made of seven amino acids, designed as a stabilized analog of tuftsin, a naturally occurring peptide fragment with immune-modulating activity. It was developed in Russia, where it has been studied and used clinically as an anxiolytic — an anti-anxiety agent — with an accompanying reputation for supporting focus and mood.

The appeal that drives interest is its profile relative to conventional anti-anxiety drugs. Unlike benzodiazepines, Selank is described as non-sedating and non-habit-forming in the available reports, which is why it is popular in nootropic circles for people who want calm without grogginess or dependence.

Honest framing: although Selank has a real history of Russian clinical use, it has limited large-scale Western trial data. It is not an FDA-approved medication and is not established as a treatment for anxiety disorders in the United States.

How Selank is thought to work

Selank appears to act through several overlapping systems rather than a single receptor. Effects described in the research literature include:

  • Enkephalin modulation — Selank is reported to slow the breakdown of enkephalins (the body's own calming peptides) by inhibiting the enzymes that degrade them, prolonging their effect.
  • GABA and serotonin influence — association with the GABAergic and serotonergic systems involved in anxiety and mood regulation.
  • BDNF support — reported effects on brain-derived neurotrophic factor, linked to its nootropic reputation.
  • Immune modulation — reflecting its tuftsin origins, Selank has been studied for effects on immune signaling such as interleukin expression.

These combined actions are the basis for its description as a calming nootropic, with the usual caveat that much of the mechanism work comes from the originating research tradition.

What the research shows

Russian clinical and animal studies have reported anxiolytic effects for Selank, including in generalized anxiety, along with effects on mood and cognition, and have emphasized the absence of the sedation and withdrawal associated with benzodiazepines. That body of work is the foundation of its reputation.

The limitation is breadth and independence: large, Western, peer-reviewed randomized trials are scarce, so the evidence is suggestive rather than definitive by those standards. Much of the enthusiast reputation for “calm without sedation” also rests on self-report, which is exactly the kind of subjective outcome that benefits from blinded, controlled study. The honest summary is “promising, with real clinical history abroad, but under-studied in the Western literature.”

How Selank is commonly used

The most common route is intranasal — drops or spray — with community and clinical references around 250-500 mcg daily. An injectable form is also used. Because it is short-acting, it is often taken as needed or split through the day rather than as a single dose.

When an injectable, lyophilized preparation is used, it has to be reconstituted with bacteriostatic water first, and the correct draw volume depends on your vial size and target dose. Because its effects are felt relatively quickly and its duration is short, Selank is often used situationally — for example before a demanding task — as well as on a steadier daily schedule, and people differ in which approach suits them. Nasal preparations should be stored per the product's guidance, and reconstituted injectable solution is generally refrigerated and used within a limited window. Commonly stacked with

Selank is very often paired with Semax, its sibling in the Russian nootropic peptide family — Selank for calm, Semax for stimulation and focus — a combination many users describe as balancing. Its amidated, longer-acting variant N-Acetyl Selank Amidate is a close relative, and the mood-oriented PE-22-28 also comes up. These are informal pairings, not validated protocols.

Side effects & safety considerations

Selank is generally described as well tolerated, non-sedating, and non-habit-forming in the available reports, with nasal irritation (from the intranasal route) being the most commonly mentioned complaint. This favorable profile is a large part of its appeal.

The caveat is that rigorous long-term human safety data from independent trials is limited, and purity varies across unregulated suppliers. Anxiety can also be a symptom of conditions that deserve proper evaluation, so self-treating is not a substitute for care. None of this is medical advice: anyone considering Selank should review it with a qualified healthcare provider who knows their history.
