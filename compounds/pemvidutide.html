What is pemvidutide?

Pemvidutide is an experimental medicine from Altimmune being developed for obesity and for MASH — metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, the fatty-liver disease formerly called NASH. It is a dual agonist: a single molecule that activates both the GLP-1 receptor and the glucagon receptor.

Adding the glucagon receptor is what distinguishes pemvidutide from GLP-1-only drugs like semaglutide. Glucagon-receptor activity influences energy expenditure and, importantly, liver fat — which is why dual GLP-1/glucagon agonists are being investigated not just for weight but specifically for fatty-liver disease. Pemvidutide's dual MASH-and-obesity program is its defining angle.

The honest status: pemvidutide is investigational. It is in clinical development, it has not been approved by any regulator, and it is not available by prescription. It is a company pipeline peptide, not a research-chemical product, so there is no established grey-market supply — anything sold under the name cannot be assumed genuine.

How pemvidutide is thought to work

Pemvidutide is designed to engage two complementary metabolic pathways from one molecule:

  • GLP-1 receptor activation — the appetite-side effect: increased satiety, slower gastric emptying, and improved blood-sugar handling, which reduce food intake.
  • Glucagon receptor activation — associated with increased energy expenditure and, of particular interest in MASH, reduced fat accumulation in the liver.
  • Combined rationale — pairing appetite suppression with a liver-and-energy mechanism is the reason this class is being tested for both weight loss and fatty-liver disease at once.

These describe the intended pharmacology of the targets; how pemvidutide performs in people is what its trials are built to measure.

What the research shows

Pemvidutide has advanced through clinical studies in both obesity and MASH, and Altimmune has reported results that kept it among the watched candidates in the dual-agonist field — with the MASH program drawing particular attention because of the glucagon mechanism's relevance to liver fat. As always for a drug in trials, the available data comes from company-reported studies still in progress, not a completed, approved evidence base.

Because the program is ongoing, quoting specific efficacy percentages, participant counts, or timelines as settled facts would be misleading. The accurate summary: a dual GLP-1/glucagon design, active programs in both obesity and MASH, reported activity in trials, and no regulatory approval — so long-term efficacy and safety are not established.

How pemvidutide is being studied

Pemvidutide is a once-weekly subcutaneous injection, typically started at a low dose and titrated upward over several weeks — the standard ramp used across the incretin class to improve tolerability. Side effects & safety considerations

As an investigational drug, pemvidutide has no established long-term human safety record. Based on the incretin class in general, the effects most commonly associated with these drugs are gastrointestinal — nausea, reduced appetite, and related digestive complaints, usually most noticeable early in treatment or after a dose increase.

The key safety point is specific to its status: pemvidutide's full risk and benefit picture will be defined by its trials and by regulators, and that process is not complete. Anything sold under its name outside a clinical trial cannot be assumed to be the real compound or correctly dosed. Pemvidutide FAQ

What is pemvidutide?

Pemvidutide is an investigational medicine from Altimmune. It is a dual agonist of the GLP-1 and glucagon receptors, given once weekly by subcutaneous injection, and is being studied for obesity and for MASH (also called NASH), a fatty-liver disease.

How is pemvidutide different from semaglutide?

Semaglutide targets the GLP-1 receptor alone. Pemvidutide adds the glucagon receptor, which is of particular interest for liver fat and metabolism. The glucagon arm is why pemvidutide is being studied in MASH as well as obesity.

What is MASH and why does pemvidutide target it?

MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, formerly NASH) is a fatty-liver disease linked to metabolic health. Glucagon-receptor activity is thought to help reduce liver fat, which is why dual GLP-1/glucagon drugs like pemvidutide are being investigated for it.

Is pemvidutide FDA approved?

No. Pemvidutide is an investigational drug in clinical trials for obesity and MASH. It has not been approved by any regulator and is not available by prescription or as a research chemical in any established way.

How is pemvidutide dosed?

Pemvidutide is being studied as a once-weekly subcutaneous injection, typically titrated upward over time, which is standard for this drug class. Specific dosing is set by trial protocols. StackMindr does not recommend doses.
