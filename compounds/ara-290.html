What is ARA-290?

ARA-290, also known by its development name cibinetide, is an 11-amino-acid peptide derived from erythropoietin (EPO) — but it is engineered to separate EPO’s two very different jobs.

Natural EPO does two things: it tells the bone marrow to make red blood cells, and, separately, it protects and repairs tissue. The blood-cell effect is useful for anemia but dangerous in excess (raising clotting and cardiovascular risk). ARA-290 is built from the part of the EPO molecule — the helix-B region — responsible for the tissue-protective and anti-inflammatory effects only. As a result it is reported to deliver EPO’s repair signaling without raising red blood cell counts, avoiding EPO’s main safety problem.

It has been studied specifically as a candidate treatment for nerve-related conditions and remains an investigational, research-stage peptide.

How ARA-290 is thought to work

Its activity is tied to a specific receptor complex rather than the classic EPO receptor:

  • Innate repair receptor (IRR) — ARA-290 selectively activates this receptor, a complex of the EPO receptor and the CD131 subunit, which drives tissue protection and repair.
  • No hematopoietic signaling — it does not meaningfully activate the classic EPO receptor that stimulates red-blood-cell production, which is the whole point of its design.
  • Anti-inflammatory effects — it dampens inflammatory signaling involved in tissue damage and neuropathic pain.
  • Nerve protection and repair — these combined effects are why it has been studied most for conditions involving damaged small nerve fibers.

By cleanly splitting repair from blood-cell stimulation, ARA-290 aims to be a tissue-protective agent you can use without EPO’s thrombotic risk.

What the research shows

ARA-290 has reached human clinical trials, most notably for small-fiber neuropathy in sarcoidosis, and has also been explored in diabetic neuropathy and neuropathic pain. In those studies it was generally well tolerated and showed encouraging signals — improvements in neuropathic symptom and pain measures and in some markers of small-nerve-fiber function.

Some of those studies tracked objective measures alongside symptoms — for example changes in small-nerve-fiber and corneal nerve measurements, and improvements in pain and quality-of-life scores — which is part of why the early signal drew attention. The compound was advanced by a dedicated developer specifically for these neuropathic indications rather than as a general wellness peptide.

The important caveat is scale: these were relatively small, early-stage trials. ARA-290 is not an approved treatment for neuropathy or anything else, and the evidence, while promising and more human-oriented than many research peptides, is not yet definitive. The honest summary is “promising early human data for nerve-related conditions, still investigational.”

How ARA-290 is commonly used

Commonly referenced figures are around 4 mg daily, given by subcutaneous injection — broadly in line with the dosing used in its clinical studies.

Side effects & safety considerations

A key safety feature of ARA-290 is what it is designed not to do: because it avoids the classic EPO receptor, it is not expected to raise red blood cell counts or carry EPO’s associated clotting and cardiovascular risks. In its trials it was generally well tolerated, with injection-site reactions the most common complaint.

That said, it remains investigational: the human data comes from small studies, there is no long-term safety record for the ways people use it outside trials, and research-supplier purity varies. This is not medical advice; anyone considering ARA-290 — particularly for a nerve condition — should do so under the care of a qualified healthcare provider.

ARA-290 FAQ

What is ARA-290?

ARA-290 (cibinetide) is an 11-amino-acid peptide derived from erythropoietin. It activates the innate repair receptor to produce EPO’s tissue-protective and anti-inflammatory effects without stimulating red-blood-cell production.

How does ARA-290 work?

It selectively activates the innate repair receptor (an EPO-receptor/CD131 complex) rather than the classic EPO receptor, driving tissue repair, anti-inflammatory effects and nerve protection without raising blood cell counts.

What is ARA-290 studied for?

Primarily small-fiber neuropathy in sarcoidosis, and also diabetic neuropathy and neuropathic pain. Early human trials were generally well tolerated and showed encouraging symptom and nerve-function signals.

Does ARA-290 raise red blood cells like EPO?

No — that is its central design feature. By avoiding the classic EPO receptor it provides EPO’s repair signaling without the increase in red blood cells or the associated clotting risk.

Is ARA-290 FDA approved?

No. ARA-290 is an investigational peptide studied in clinical trials and is not an approved treatment for any condition.

How is ARA-290 administered?

Research figures reference around 4 mg daily by subcutaneous injection, in line with its clinical studies. This is educational reference only; dosing should be set with a qualified healthcare provider.
