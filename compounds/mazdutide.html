What is mazdutide?

Mazdutide — development code IBI362 — is a dual GLP-1 / glucagon receptor agonist, a single engineered peptide that activates two receptors at once. It is being developed by Innovent Biologics in partnership with Eli Lilly, and it belongs to the same multi-agonist wave of metabolic drugs as compounds like survodutide and retatrutide.

What's distinctive about mazdutide is its origin: it is an acylated, long-acting analog of oxyntomodulin, a naturally occurring gut hormone that already engages both the GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. Rather than fusing two separate signals, mazdutide is built on a molecule that is dual-acting by nature.

Its regulatory status is unusual for this list: mazdutide was approved in China in 2025 for weight management and diabetes, making it one of the first GLP-1/glucagon dual agonists to reach approval anywhere. Outside China, including in the United States, it remains investigational and is not approved.

How mazdutide is thought to work

Mazdutide works by reproducing oxyntomodulin's dual-receptor activity in a form that lasts long enough for once-weekly dosing:

  • GLP-1 receptor activation — reduces appetite, slows gastric emptying, and supports glucose-dependent insulin release, driving weight loss and better blood-sugar control.
  • Glucagon receptor activation — associated with increased energy expenditure and with effects on fat metabolism, including in the liver.
  • Oxyntomodulin-based design — because it is modeled on a hormone that naturally hits both receptors, the two activities are built into one molecule rather than engineered as a fusion.

The combination is intended to add the energy-expenditure dimension of glucagon on top of the appetite-and-glucose effects of GLP-1, which is the general thesis behind the whole dual-agonist category.

What the research shows

Mazdutide has been studied primarily for weight management and type 2 diabetes, and that body of clinical work supported its 2025 approval in China. That approval is a meaningful marker — it means mazdutide has cleared a regulatory bar in at least one major market, which sets it apart from purely investigational compounds that have only trial data.

At the same time, its status elsewhere is still investigational, and approval in one country does not equal approval globally or guarantee the same label everywhere. The honest summary is that mazdutide is an advanced, clinically studied dual agonist with real regulatory traction in China, while its wider availability and long-term, cross-population safety picture continue to develop. As always, treat precise efficacy figures in context rather than as universal claims.

How mazdutide is commonly used

Mazdutide is a once-weekly subcutaneous injection, with doses titrated upward over time to improve tolerability. In its investigational and compounded form it is typically supplied as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder that must be reconstituted with bacteriostatic water before it can be drawn and injected.

Commonly compared with

Mazdutide sits in the multi-receptor family and is most often compared with its peers. Survodutide is the other prominent GLP-1/glucagon dual agonist; retatrutide extends the idea to a triple GLP-1/GIP/glucagon agonist; and tirzepatide is the approved GLP-1/GIP dual agonist that set the commercial benchmark for the category.

Side effects & safety considerations

As with other agents acting on these pathways, the effects most associated with mazdutide are gastrointestinal — nausea, reduced appetite, and related digestive complaints — most prominent early and during dose increases, which is why dosing is titrated gradually. The glucagon component also brings considerations around heart rate and metabolic parameters that trials monitor.

The key caveats are jurisdiction and sourcing. Mazdutide's approval is specific to China; it is not approved in the United States, so material obtained outside that approved supply chain — grey-market or compounded — carries added uncertainty about purity and labeling, and is not backed by the same oversight. None of this is medical advice. Anyone considering mazdutide should review it with a qualified healthcare provider who knows their history.

Mazdutide FAQ

What is mazdutide?

Mazdutide (development code IBI362) is a dual GLP-1 / glucagon receptor agonist. It is an acylated analog of oxyntomodulin, a natural gut hormone, and is developed by Innovent Biologics in partnership with Eli Lilly. It is a once-weekly subcutaneous injection.

Is mazdutide approved?

Mazdutide was approved in China in 2025 for weight management and diabetes. It remains investigational in the United States and most other markets, where it is not approved.

How does mazdutide work?

As a dual agonist, it activates both the GLP-1 receptor and the glucagon receptor. The GLP-1 arm reduces appetite and slows gastric emptying, while the glucagon arm is associated with increased energy expenditure and effects on fat metabolism. It is modeled on oxyntomodulin, which naturally engages both receptors.

What is oxyntomodulin?

Oxyntomodulin is a naturally occurring gut hormone that activates both the GLP-1 and glucagon receptors. Mazdutide is an engineered, long-acting analog of oxyntomodulin, which is why it shares that dual-receptor activity.

How is mazdutide administered?

How is mazdutide different from survodutide?

Both are dual GLP-1 / glucagon receptor agonists in the same class. They are different molecules from different developers — mazdutide is an oxyntomodulin analog from Innovent and Eli Lilly, while survodutide is from Boehringer Ingelheim. Their approval status also differs.
