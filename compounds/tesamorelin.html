What is Tesamorelin?

Tesamorelin is a synthetic analog of growth-hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH), based on the full 44-amino-acid hormone with a chemical modification (a trans-3-hexenoic acid group) that makes it far more stable against the enzymes that normally break GHRH down. That stability is what turned a short-lived natural signal into a viable, once-daily injectable drug.

It is the standout in the GHRH-analog family because it is a genuine, FDA-approved medicine. Sold under the brand name Egrifta, tesamorelin is approved to reduce excess visceral abdominal fat in people with HIV-associated lipodystrophy — a condition in which deep abdominal fat accumulates. That approval, backed by large clinical trials, is why tesamorelin has a credibility and an evidence base that most peptides in this space simply do not.

Interest in tesamorelin now extends well beyond its approved indication, largely because of that visceral-fat data. Any use outside the HIV-lipodystrophy indication is off-label and not formally established, and this page is educational reference only.

How Tesamorelin is thought to work

Tesamorelin works upstream of growth hormone, at the pituitary, through the GHRH receptor:

  • GHRH-receptor agonism — it binds the GHRH receptor on pituitary somatotroph cells, stimulating the synthesis and release of the body's own growth hormone.
  • Preserved pulsatility and feedback — because it acts upstream, growth hormone is still released in pulses and remains under the control of somatostatin and IGF-1 feedback, rather than being delivered as a flat, external supply.
  • Downstream IGF-1 — the released growth hormone raises IGF-1, the mediator behind growth hormone's effects on metabolism and tissue.
  • Visceral-fat reduction — in clinical studies this GH axis stimulation was associated specifically with a reduction in visceral adipose tissue, the deep fat around the organs, which is the basis of its approved use.

This upstream mechanism is shared with sermorelin and CJC-1295, but tesamorelin is the one whose fat-reduction effect has been demonstrated in large trials.

What the research shows

Tesamorelin has the strongest clinical evidence of any compound on this list. Its FDA approval rests on large, randomized, placebo-controlled trials in people with HIV-associated lipodystrophy, which showed that daily tesamorelin meaningfully reduced visceral abdominal fat and raised IGF-1, with benefits that reversed when treatment stopped. That is a real, replicated, drug-grade result — not an anecdote.

Research has also explored tesamorelin in other contexts, such as fatty liver and cognition, with varying and more preliminary results. What has not been established is its use as a general body-composition or "anti-aging" tool in otherwise healthy adults — that is off-label and not backed by the kind of outcome trials that support the approved indication. The honest framing: a proven drug for a specific population, with popular interest running ahead of the evidence for everyone else.

How Tesamorelin is commonly used

In its approved medical use, tesamorelin is given as 2 mg once daily by subcutaneous injection; commonly referenced ranges fall around 1-2 mg daily, usually taken at bedtime so the growth-hormone pulse lines up with the body's largest natural overnight release. Unlike the micrograms used for sermorelin or ipamorelin, tesamorelin is dosed in milligrams.

It is supplied as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder that must be reconstituted before subcutaneous injection. Commonly stacked with

Tesamorelin is a potent GHRH analog on its own, so it is less often "stacked" than the lighter peptides. When people do combine GH peptides, the usual logic is to pair a GHRH analog with a growth-hormone-releasing peptide such as ipamorelin, which acts on a different receptor. Within the GHRH-analog family, CJC-1295 no DAC and sermorelin are the compounds most often compared to tesamorelin.

Side effects & safety considerations

Because tesamorelin has been through large trials, its side-effect profile is better documented than most peptides. The most commonly reported effects are injection-site reactions (redness, itching, swelling), joint pain, muscle aches, swelling in the extremities, and tingling or numbness — effects consistent with raising growth hormone. Because it raises IGF-1 and can affect glucose handling, blood sugar should be monitored, particularly in people with or at risk of diabetes.

Important cautions carried in its prescribing information: tesamorelin is contraindicated in pregnancy, in people with active malignancy, and in those with disruption of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis; growth-hormone stimulation is a concern where tumor growth is a risk. It is approved only for HIV-associated visceral fat, and its effect reverses when stopped. None of this is medical advice — tesamorelin is a prescription medication that affects a hormonal axis and should only be used under the supervision of a qualified healthcare provider who knows your history.

Tesamorelin FAQ

What is a common Tesamorelin dose?

The FDA-approved dose for its medical indication is 2 mg once daily by subcutaneous injection; commonly referenced ranges fall around 1-2 mg daily, usually at bedtime. This is educational reference only — dosing should be set with a qualified healthcare provider.

Is Tesamorelin FDA approved?

Yes. Tesamorelin (brand name Egrifta) is FDA-approved to reduce excess visceral abdominal fat in people with HIV-associated lipodystrophy. Any other use is off-label and not established.

What is Tesamorelin known for?

It is the GHRH analog with the strongest clinical evidence for reducing visceral (deep abdominal) fat, which is why it draws interest beyond its approved indication. It works by prompting the body's own pulsatile growth-hormone release.

How do you reconstitute Tesamorelin?

Tesamorelin is supplied as a lyophilized powder and reconstituted before subcutaneous injection. StackMindr's free reconstitution calculator gives you the exact units to draw for your vial size and target dose on a U-100 insulin syringe.

How is Tesamorelin different from Sermorelin?

Both are GHRH analogs that signal the pituitary to release growth hormone. Tesamorelin is a stabilized molecule with an FDA approval and the strongest trial evidence for visceral-fat reduction, and is dosed in milligrams; sermorelin is dosed in micrograms and is used mainly through compounding pharmacies.

When do people take Tesamorelin?

Once daily, commonly at bedtime, so the growth-hormone pulse aligns with the body's largest natural overnight release. It is given as a subcutaneous injection.
