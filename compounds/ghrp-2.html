What is GHRP-2?

GHRP-2 stands for growth hormone-releasing peptide 2. It belongs to a family of synthetic peptides known as growth hormone secretagogues — compounds that prompt the pituitary gland to release a pulse of the body's own growth hormone, rather than adding synthetic growth hormone from outside.

What makes GHRP-2 distinctive is where it acts. It stimulates the ghrelin receptor — the same receptor used by ghrelin, the natural "hunger hormone" — which is why it both triggers GH release and tends to stimulate appetite. It is considered a potent, reliable GH-releaser, and it has been studied clinically as a way to probe pituitary function.

GHRP-2 is often compared with Ipamorelin, a gentler, more selective secretagogue. The trade-off is characteristic of this class: GHRP-2 typically delivers a stronger GH pulse but also raises other hormones more. It is not an approved drug for general use, it is sold as a research compound, and it is banned in competitive sport.

How GHRP-2 is thought to work

The mechanisms are well characterized for this class of peptide. The effects most often described include:

  • Ghrelin-receptor activation — GHRP-2 binds the GH secretagogue (ghrelin) receptor on the pituitary and hypothalamus, driving a pulse of growth hormone release.
  • Amplified natural pulsatility — because it prompts the body's own GH, the release follows a natural pulse-and-recover pattern rather than a flat, constant elevation.
  • Appetite stimulation — acting on the ghrelin system also tends to increase hunger, which can be useful for those trying to gain weight and inconvenient for those cutting.
  • Secondary hormone rise — GHRP-2 is known to produce a larger increase in cortisol and prolactin than more selective peptides, a key reason it is often described as "stronger but less clean."

Many protocols pair a GHRP like this with a GHRH analog (such as a CJC peptide), since the two classes act on different receptors and are often described as synergistic for GH release.

What the research shows

GHRP-2 is one of the better-studied secretagogues. Clinical research has confirmed that it reliably stimulates growth hormone release in humans, and it has been used as a diagnostic tool to test pituitary GH reserve. Its effects on GH and the downstream marker IGF-1 are reasonably well documented.

Where evidence is thinner is on the long-term outcomes people actually use it for — body composition, recovery, anti-aging. Raising GH acutely is established; proving durable, meaningful physique or health benefits from chronic use, with a clean long-term safety record, is not. The honest summary is that the mechanism is real and well demonstrated, while the long-run risk-benefit picture for lifestyle use remains under-characterized.

How GHRP-2 is commonly used

GHRP-2 is supplied as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder that is reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and injected subcutaneously. Commonly referenced protocols use around 100-300 mcg per dose, two to three times a day — classic timing being on waking, pre-workout, and before bed, to echo the body's natural GH pulses. There is no official protocol behind these numbers.

A common convention is to dose fasted, with a window before eating, because food — especially carbohydrates and fats — blunts the GH response. Commonly stacked with

GHRP-2 is most often paired with a GHRH analog such as CJC-1295 (no DAC), since combining a GHRP with a GHRH is widely described as producing a larger, more synergistic GH pulse than either alone. Ipamorelin comes up as the gentler GHRP alternative for those who want less cortisol and prolactin, and GHRP-6 as the closely related sibling with even stronger appetite effects.

Side effects & safety considerations

The side effects most often reported with GHRP-2 follow from its mechanism: increased hunger, water retention, tingling or numbness in the hands, and injection-site reactions. Because it raises cortisol and prolactin more than selective peptides, some users report related effects, and the sharp appetite increase can work against fat-loss goals.

The broader caution is that any compound raising growth hormone and IGF-1 carries theoretical long-term concerns — effects on insulin sensitivity and blood sugar, and the general principle that promoting growth signaling is not advisable for anyone with a history of cancer. Purity also varies across unregulated suppliers, and GHRP-2 is a prohibited substance in tested sport. None of this is medical advice — anyone considering GHRP-2 should review it with a qualified healthcare provider who knows their history.

GHRP-2 FAQ

What is GHRP-2?

GHRP-2 (growth hormone-releasing peptide 2) is a synthetic peptide that stimulates the pituitary gland to release a pulse of the body's own growth hormone. It works through the ghrelin receptor, the same receptor the natural hunger hormone uses.

How is GHRP-2 different from Ipamorelin?

Both are growth hormone secretagogues, but GHRP-2 tends to produce a stronger GH pulse while also causing a larger rise in cortisol and prolactin and more hunger. Ipamorelin is considered more selective, with less effect on those other hormones.

What is a common GHRP-2 dose?

Commonly referenced protocols use around 100-300 mcg per dose, often 2-3 times daily — for example on waking, pre-workout, and before bed. This is educational reference only and not a recommendation.

Why is GHRP-2 taken on an empty stomach?

Food — particularly carbohydrates and fats — blunts the growth hormone response to secretagogues. Dosing fasted, with a window before eating, is a common convention aimed at getting a cleaner GH pulse.

Is GHRP-2 FDA approved?

No. GHRP-2 is not an approved drug for general use and is sold as a research compound. It is not a treatment for any condition, and it is a banned substance in competitive sport.
