What is VK2735?

VK2735 is an experimental medicine being developed by Viking Therapeutics for obesity and related metabolic conditions. It belongs to the same broad family as the well-known incretin drugs: it activates two gut-hormone receptors, GLP-1 and GIP. That is the same receptor pair targeted by tirzepatide, which is why VK2735 is often described as a tirzepatide-class molecule — though it is a distinct, separately engineered compound, not a copy or generic of any approved drug.

What makes VK2735 notable in the development pipeline is that Viking has pursued it in two formats at once: a once-weekly subcutaneous injection and an oral tablet. Most incretin drugs started as injections, so an oral version in the same program has drawn particular attention.

The honest framing is important: VK2735 is investigational. It is in clinical development, it has not been approved by the FDA or any other regulator, and it is not available by prescription. It also is not something with an established grey-market or research-chemical supply chain the way older research peptides are — availability is limited, and purity and identity of anything sold under its name cannot be assumed.

How VK2735 is thought to work

As a dual incretin receptor agonist, VK2735 is designed to mimic two naturally occurring gut hormones at once:

  • GLP-1 receptor activation — associated with increased satiety, slower gastric emptying, and improved blood-sugar regulation, which together tend to reduce food intake.
  • GIP receptor activation — a second incretin pathway thought to complement GLP-1 signaling and influence how the body handles nutrients and stores fat.
  • Combined effect — the rationale for dual agonists is that engaging both receptors may produce greater metabolic and weight effects than targeting GLP-1 alone, which is the thesis behind tirzepatide and the drugs following it.

These mechanisms describe the drug class in general. How VK2735 specifically performs in people is exactly what its clinical trials are measuring.

What the research shows

VK2735 has moved through early and mid-stage human studies, with its injectable program reporting weight-loss signals that drew significant interest, and its oral program also advancing in trials. In honest terms, the data available is from company-reported clinical trials still in progress, not from a completed, approved body of evidence.

Because the program is ongoing, it would be misleading to quote precise efficacy percentages, trial sizes, or timelines as settled facts. What can be said plainly is that VK2735 is one of several next-generation incretin candidates being watched closely, that early results have been considered encouraging by its developer, and that long-term efficacy and safety have not been established through approval.

How VK2735 is being developed

Two routes are in play:

  • Subcutaneous injection — studied as a once-weekly dose titrated upward over time, the standard pattern for this class. An injectable peptide like this is supplied as a solution or as a lyophilized powder that would be reconstituted before use.
  • Oral tablet — a once-daily pill, which removes injections and refrigeration from the picture entirely.

Side effects & safety considerations

Because VK2735 is still investigational, there is no established long-term human safety record. Based on how the GLP-1/GIP class behaves in general, the effects most commonly associated with these drugs are gastrointestinal — nausea, reduced appetite, and related digestive complaints, typically most noticeable when dosing is increased.

The most important safety point is the one specific to an experimental drug: its full risk profile is not yet known, and any product sold under the VK2735 name outside a clinical trial cannot be assumed to be the real compound, correctly dosed, or pure. VK2735 FAQ

What is VK2735?

VK2735 is an investigational dual agonist of the GLP-1 and GIP receptors being developed by Viking Therapeutics for obesity and metabolic disease. It targets the same receptor pair as tirzepatide and is being studied in both a weekly subcutaneous injection and an oral tablet.

Is VK2735 the same as tirzepatide?

No. VK2735 is a different, separately developed molecule, but it acts on the same two receptors as tirzepatide — GLP-1 and GIP. It is not a generic or copy of tirzepatide and is still investigational.

Is VK2735 available in a pill?

Viking Therapeutics is developing VK2735 in two forms: a once-weekly subcutaneous injection and an oral tablet. Both are in clinical development and neither is approved or commercially available.

Is VK2735 FDA approved?

No. VK2735 is an investigational drug in clinical trials. It has not been approved by the FDA or any other regulator, and it is not available by prescription or as a research chemical in any established way.

How is injectable VK2735 dosed?

The injectable form is being studied as a once-weekly subcutaneous dose that is titrated upward over time, which is the typical pattern for this drug class. Specific dosing is set by trial protocols and is not something to self-administer. StackMindr does not recommend doses.
