What is Ipamorelin?

Ipamorelin is a synthetic pentapeptide — just five amino acids — in the class known as growth-hormone-releasing peptides (GHRPs), also called growth-hormone secretagogues. Rather than mimicking GHRH, it mimics ghrelin, the "hunger hormone," and acts on the same receptor ghrelin uses to prompt the pituitary to release growth hormone.

What made ipamorelin stand out when it was developed is its selectivity. Earlier GHRPs such as GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 reliably release growth hormone, but they also tend to spike appetite and can nudge cortisol and prolactin upward. Ipamorelin was designed to trigger a strong, clean growth-hormone pulse while leaving those other hormones largely untouched at typical doses. That cleaner profile is the single biggest reason it became the default GHRP in this space.

Like the other peptides in this family, ipamorelin is used and discussed as a research compound. It is not an approved medicine and is not established as a treatment for any condition.

How Ipamorelin is thought to work

Ipamorelin acts at the pituitary through the growth-hormone secretagogue (ghrelin) receptor:

  • GHS-receptor agonism — it binds the ghrelin/GHS receptor on pituitary cells, directly stimulating a pulse of growth-hormone release.
  • Selectivity — at typical doses it releases growth hormone without the meaningful rises in cortisol and prolactin seen with older GHRPs, and with little of the sharp appetite stimulation ghrelin itself causes.
  • Short, pulsatile action — it is cleared quickly, so it produces a discrete burst rather than a sustained elevation, which is considered closer to the body's natural pattern.
  • Complementary to GHRH analogs — because it works on a different receptor than GHRH analogs, combining the two is thought to produce a larger pulse than either alone.

This is the mechanistic basis for ipamorelin's reputation as a "gentle" GH peptide and for its near-universal pairing with a GHRH analog.

What the research shows

The GHRP/ghrelin-receptor pathway is well characterized in endocrinology, and early research on ipamorelin established that it reliably releases growth hormone with a favorable selectivity profile — that is where its reputation comes from. The broader secretagogue class has also been studied clinically; for example, related oral ghrelin-receptor agonists have been developed and investigated as drugs, which lends mechanistic credibility to the approach.

Ipamorelin itself, however, has not been through large human trials for the body-composition, recovery, sleep, or longevity outcomes it is popularly associated with. Its effect on growth-hormone release is real and well documented; the specific downstream benefits people pursue in healthy adults are largely extrapolated. The honest summary is "well-characterized GH release, limited human outcome data" — treat sweeping transformation claims with skepticism.

How Ipamorelin is commonly used

Commonly referenced protocols use 100-300 mcg per dose, most often once daily at bedtime and on an empty stomach. Bedtime is chosen so the pulse stacks on the body's largest natural overnight GH release, and the empty-stomach guidance exists because carbohydrate and fat in a recent meal can blunt the response. Because ipamorelin is short-acting, some protocols reference dosing two to three times a day, spaced away from meals.

It comes as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder that must be reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and injected subcutaneously with a small insulin syringe. Commonly stacked with

Ipamorelin and CJC-1295 no DAC are the most famous pairing in the GH-peptide world. Ipamorelin is a GHRP and CJC-1295 no DAC is a GHRH analog, so they act on two different receptors and are described as synergistic — the GHRH analog increases the size of the pulse ipamorelin triggers. The combination is sold so often that it also appears as a pre-mixed CJC/Ipamorelin blend. Sermorelin is another GHRH analog people pair with it for the same reason.

Side effects & safety considerations

In anecdotal reports and the limited available data, ipamorelin is generally described as one of the better-tolerated GH peptides, which is part of its appeal. The most commonly mentioned effects are injection-site reactions (redness, itching), transient flushing or a head-rush feeling shortly after dosing, mild water retention or tingling in the hands, and occasional headache. Its selectivity means it tends to avoid the strong hunger surge and cortisol/prolactin bump associated with older GHRPs.

The honest limitations: there is no long-term human safety record for ipamorelin, and because it raises growth hormone and IGF-1, anyone with active cancer, a history of cancer, or conditions sensitive to IGF-1 signaling is generally advised to be especially cautious. Purity and dosing accuracy vary across research suppliers of an unregulated product. None of this is medical advice — ipamorelin affects a hormonal axis and should only be considered under the supervision of a qualified healthcare provider who knows your history.

Ipamorelin FAQ

What is a common Ipamorelin dose?

Commonly referenced protocols use 100-300 mcg once daily at bedtime, sometimes more often because it is short-acting. This is educational reference only — dosing should be set with a qualified healthcare provider.

Why is Ipamorelin considered selective?

Ipamorelin stimulates growth-hormone release through the ghrelin/GHS receptor but, unlike older GHRPs, does so with little effect on cortisol, prolactin, or appetite at typical doses. That cleaner profile is the main reason it is so widely used.

Why is Ipamorelin stacked with CJC-1295?

Ipamorelin is a GHRP and CJC-1295 no DAC is a GHRH analog, so they act on two different receptors. Used together they are described as synergistic — the GHRH analog increases the size of the GH pulse that ipamorelin triggers — which is why the pair is so popular.

How do you reconstitute Ipamorelin?

Ipamorelin is supplied as a lyophilized powder and mixed with bacteriostatic water before subcutaneous injection. StackMindr's free reconstitution calculator gives you the exact units to draw for your vial size and target dose on a U-100 insulin syringe.

Is Ipamorelin FDA approved?

No. Ipamorelin is not an approved drug; it remains a research compound. Human data is limited and it is not a treatment for any condition. Discuss any use with a qualified healthcare provider.

When do people take Ipamorelin?

Bedtime on an empty stomach is the most commonly referenced timing, so the pulse aligns with the body's largest natural overnight GH release. Because it is short-acting, some protocols reference dosing more than once a day, away from meals containing fat or carbohydrate.
