What is survodutide?

Survodutide is an investigational dual receptor agonist — a single engineered peptide designed to activate two different receptors at once: the GLP-1 receptor and the glucagon receptor. It is being developed by Boehringer Ingelheim and sits in the newer wave of "multi-agonist" metabolic drugs that try to improve on single-target GLP-1 agents by recruiting more than one pathway.

What makes survodutide stand out within that wave is its target set. The glucagon arm gives it a mechanism of particular interest to the liver, which is why survodutide is being studied not only for obesity but also for MASH — metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, a progressive form of fatty liver disease previously known as NASH.

The honest framing: survodutide is investigational and not approved. It is being evaluated in clinical trials, and its evidence should be read as emerging rather than established. It is not a treatment for any condition.

How survodutide is thought to work

Survodutide's rationale rests on combining two complementary hormone pathways in one molecule:

  • GLP-1 receptor activation — the familiar appetite-and-glucose arm: reduced appetite, slowed gastric emptying, and glucose-dependent insulin support, which together drive weight loss and better blood-sugar control.
  • Glucagon receptor activation — associated with increased energy expenditure and with effects on fat metabolism in the liver. In the right balance, glucagon signaling is thought to help mobilize and reduce liver fat.
  • Combined effect — pairing the two is intended to produce more weight loss than GLP-1 alone while adding a direct metabolic action on the liver, which is the basis for studying it in both obesity and MASH.

The balance between the GLP-1 and glucagon activity is central to how these dual agonists behave, and characterizing that balance in humans is a key aim of the ongoing trials.

What the research shows

Survodutide has progressed through clinical development on two fronts. In obesity, it has been studied for its effect on body weight; in MASH/NASH, it has been evaluated for its effect on the liver, where the glucagon component is the point of interest. Trials in these areas have been reported in stages as the program advances.

What's fair to say is that the GLP-1/glucagon dual-agonist concept is an active and credible area of metabolic drug development, and survodutide is one of the notable candidates pursuing the liver application specifically. What's not yet settled is its final efficacy and long-term safety, which depend on larger, later-stage results. Treat precise figures from early trials as provisional rather than final, and be skeptical of sources presenting investigational data as if it were an approved label.

How survodutide is commonly used

In clinical trials survodutide is a once-weekly subcutaneous injection, with doses titrated upward over time to manage tolerability. In its investigational and compounded form it is typically supplied as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder that must be reconstituted with bacteriostatic water before it can be drawn and injected.

Commonly compared with

Survodutide belongs to the multi-receptor family, so it is most often compared with its peers rather than stacked. Retatrutide goes a step further as a triple GLP-1/GIP/glucagon agonist; tirzepatide is a GLP-1/GIP dual agonist that is already approved; and semaglutide is the single-receptor GLP-1 benchmark the whole category is measured against. Mazdutide is another GLP-1/glucagon dual agonist in the same specific class as survodutide.

Side effects & safety considerations

As with other agents in this class, the effects most associated with survodutide in trials are gastrointestinal — nausea, reduced appetite, and related digestive complaints — most prominent early and during dose increases, which is why dosing is titrated gradually. The glucagon arm also introduces considerations around heart rate and metabolic parameters that trials monitor closely.

The central limitation is that survodutide is investigational: there is no long-term, approved safety record, and grey-market or compounded material adds uncertainty about purity and labeling. None of this is medical advice. Anyone considering survodutide should review it with a qualified healthcare provider who knows their history — this is especially true given its study in liver disease, a context where self-directed use is risky.

Survodutide FAQ

What is survodutide?

Survodutide is an investigational dual agonist that activates both the GLP-1 receptor and the glucagon receptor. It is being developed by Boehringer Ingelheim and studied for obesity and for MASH/NASH liver disease. It is a once-weekly subcutaneous injection and is not approved.

How does survodutide work?

It acts on two receptors at once. The GLP-1 arm reduces appetite and slows gastric emptying, while the glucagon arm is associated with increased energy expenditure and effects on liver fat metabolism. Combining the two is the rationale for studying it in both weight and liver disease.

What is survodutide being studied for?

Survodutide is in clinical development for two main areas: obesity and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH, previously called NASH), a form of fatty liver disease. The glucagon component is of particular interest for the liver.

Is survodutide FDA approved?

No. Survodutide is investigational and not approved by the FDA or other major regulators. Its evidence comes from clinical trials that are still ongoing. It is not a treatment for any condition.

How is survodutide administered?

Survodutide is a once-weekly subcutaneous injection. Research and compounded material is typically supplied as a lyophilized powder that is reconstituted with bacteriostatic water before use. StackMindr's free reconstitution calculator gives the exact units to draw.

How is survodutide different from tirzepatide?

Both are dual agonists, but they hit different second receptors. Tirzepatide combines GLP-1 with GIP, while survodutide combines GLP-1 with glucagon. The glucagon arm is why survodutide is being studied specifically for liver disease as well as weight.
