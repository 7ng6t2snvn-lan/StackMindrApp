Best GLP-1 Tracker App

If you're on semaglutide, tirzepatide, or another GLP-1, the right app keeps your weekly dose on schedule, your weight trend in view, and your supply from running out. Here's what to look for — and an honest comparison of the leading options.

The short answer

The best GLP-1 tracker is simply the one that does the whole job in one place: weekly reminders that respect a titration schedule, injection-site rotation, weight and measurement trends, supply tracking so you reorder in time, a cost view, and somewhere to log side effects and labs. For most people on iOS or Android, StackMindr covers all of that with a generous free tier. Regimen and OptiPin are strong alternatives depending on your platform and priorities. Below is how to choose, and how the three compare.

What to look for in a GLP-1 tracker

  • Titration-aware reminders. GLP-1s are dosed weekly and stepped up over time. Good reminders handle the schedule and the changing dose, not just a static alarm.
  • Weight & measurement trends. Seeing your dose history and your weight on one timeline is the single most useful view — it shows whether things are actually moving.
  • Injection-site rotation. A simple way to log and rotate where you inject.
  • Supply & cost. Tracking vials or pens so you reorder before running out, and ideally what the plan costs per month.
  • Side-effect & lab logging. Somewhere to note nausea or other effects, and to keep lab results if you get bloodwork.
  • Platform & extras. Does it run on your phone? Bonus points for a home-screen widget and health-data sync.

Why track a GLP-1 at all?

GLP-1 medicines work best with consistency, and the parts that are easy to lose track of are exactly the ones a tracker handles: taking your dose on the right day, stepping up at the right time, rotating injection sites, and reordering before you run out. Logging your weight alongside your doses turns "I think it's working" into a trend you can actually see — and gives you something concrete to bring to your prescriber.

None of this replaces medical care. You and your provider decide your dose and plan; a good app just helps you follow it and see how it's going.
