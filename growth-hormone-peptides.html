Growth Hormone Peptides

Instead of injecting synthetic HGH, growth hormone peptides nudge your own pituitary to release more of its own. This guide explains the two families — GHRH analogs and secretagogues — how they're combined, and what each one is known for.

What are growth hormone peptides?

Growth hormone peptides are compounds that increase the body's own output of growth hormone, rather than replacing it the way injectable HGH does. The appeal, in theory, is a more natural, pulsing release that works with the body's existing feedback loops instead of overriding them.

They split into two families that work through two different receptors:

  • GHRH analogs — copies of growth-hormone-releasing hormone. They raise the underlying signal telling the pituitary to release GH. Examples: sermorelin, CJC-1295, tesamorelin.
  • Secretagogues / GHRPs — copies of ghrelin (the "hunger hormone"). They trigger a GH pulse through a separate receptor. Examples: ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, hexarelin.

Because the two families act on different pathways, they're frequently combined — a GHRH analog plus a GHRP produces a larger, more natural pulse than either on its own. That's the logic behind the well-known CJC-1295 + ipamorelin pairing.

These are lyophilized peptides, reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and injected subcutaneously — commonly referenced in protocols timed around sleep or training, when natural GH release already peaks. Growth Hormone Peptides FAQ

What are growth hormone peptides?

Compounds that prompt the body to release its own growth hormone rather than injecting synthetic HGH. They fall into two families: GHRH analogs (sermorelin, CJC-1295, tesamorelin) that mimic growth-hormone-releasing hormone, and secretagogues or GHRPs (ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, hexarelin) that mimic ghrelin. Both act on the pituitary to increase GH output.

What is the difference between a GHRH analog and a GHRP?

GHRH analogs raise the baseline signal for GH release; GHRPs trigger a GH pulse through a different (ghrelin) receptor. Because they work through two pathways, the two are often combined — the classic example is CJC-1295 with ipamorelin — for a larger, more natural pulse than either alone.

Which growth hormone peptide is the cleanest?

Ipamorelin is widely described as the most selective — a GH pulse with minimal effect on cortisol, prolactin, or appetite, which is why it's a common starting point and the usual partner for CJC-1295. GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 are stronger but raise appetite and other hormones more; hexarelin is the most potent but most prone to desensitization.

Are growth hormone peptides FDA approved?

Mostly no. Sermorelin and tesamorelin have approved medical uses (tesamorelin for HIV-associated lipodystrophy), but most — CJC-1295, ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, hexarelin — are research compounds not approved as anti-aging or performance treatments. They are not a substitute for medical care.
